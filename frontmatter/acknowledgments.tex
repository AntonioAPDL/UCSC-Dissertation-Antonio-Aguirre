\begin{acknowledgements}

This dissertation incorporates material from four collaborative research
projects. The associated manuscripts use the scholarly name Antonio de Leon and
list the candidate as first author.

Chapter~\ref{ch:research-b} adapts material from \emph{Bayesian Quantile-Based
Correction and Synthesis of Hydrologic Products}, coauthored with Raquel Prado
and Bruno Sans\'o \citep{qdesn-DeLeonPradoSanso2026HydrologicProducts}. The
candidate is first and corresponding author. The chapter's dissertation
contribution is the
source-aware correction and synthesis model, its variational approximation, and
the San Lorenzo River study.

Chapter~\ref{ch:research-a} adapts material from \emph{exdqlm: An R Package for
Estimation and Analysis of Flexible Dynamic Quantile Linear Models}, coauthored
with Raquel Barata, Raquel Prado, and Bruno Sans\'o
\citep{env-deleon2026exdqlm}. The \pkg{exdqlm} package metadata also lists
Antonio de Leon as an author. His contribution is the substantial later
restructuring and extension of the package: improved Laplace--Delta
variational and nonconjugate scale--skewness updates; compiled implementations
for intensive operations, with \proglang{R} implementations retained where
applicable; filtering and smoothing corrections; forecasting, diagnostics,
scoring, and posterior-predictive synthesis; and static asymmetric-Laplace and
extended-asymmetric-Laplace regression with regularized-horseshoe shrinkage.
The chapter credits the distributions, model, and original algorithms on which
this work builds.

Chapter~\ref{ch:research-c} adapts material from \emph{Bayesian Quantile
Regression with Deep Echo State Network Features}, coauthored with Raquel Prado
and Bruno Sans\'o \citep{qdesn-deleon2026qdesn}. The project develops the
single-level and multiquantile formulations, their posterior computations, and
the simulation, GloFAS, and PriceFM studies presented in the chapter.

Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d} combines material from \emph{Mean-Tilted Intervals:
Short Tolerance Intervals} and \emph{Mean-Tilted Intervals: Regression and
Dynamic Models}, both coauthored with Raquel Prado and Bruno Sans\'o
\citep{rqr-deleon2026short,rqr-deleon2026extensions}. The project characterizes
the retained-mean property of the mean-preserving target, develops the
mean-tilted interval family and calibrated profiled tolerance procedure with
its empirical assessment, and proposes regression and dynamic endpoint
extensions. The chapter credits the antecedent residual-product criterion and
population-content result on which these contributions build.

AI-assisted tools were used in a limited supporting role for selected coding
debugging and for checks of grammar, readability, organization, and
consistency. They were not used for full coding implementation, formulation of
the statistical methodology, mathematical derivations or proofs, literature
searches or evaluation, independent execution of the research analyses, or
generation of the reported results. Suggestions from these tools were treated
as drafts rather than as authoritative output. The author reviewed and
verified all code changes, references, mathematical statements, analyses,
interpretations, and final text and takes responsibility for the content of
the dissertation.

\end{acknowledgements}
