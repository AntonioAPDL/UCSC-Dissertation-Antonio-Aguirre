\chapter{Statistical targets and conventions}
\label{app:shared-conventions}

The research chapters share some computational representations but have
different inferential targets. This appendix is a concordance, not a common
model: similar augmentation formulas do not imply inferential equivalence.

\section{Targets and update mechanisms}

Chapters~\ref{ch:research-b}--\ref{ch:research-c} estimate conditional
response quantiles under AL or exAL working likelihoods. An interval for a
quantile location, coefficient, or latent state describes posterior
uncertainty in that fitted object. A predictive interval additionally
integrates the observation component of the stated model. Neither object is
a tolerance interval without a separate repeated-sampling construction.

Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d} instead treats a fixed-content interval as the
estimand. Its content \(c\) specifies the probability window, its tilt
\(\delta\) specifies placement through a retained-mean condition, and its
generalized-Bayes learning rate controls concentration of the loss update.
The pseudo-AL representation used for computation augments the product loss;
it is not a response likelihood for either endpoint.

\section{AL and exAL latent-variable scales}

Chapters~\ref{ch:research-a} and~\ref{ch:research-c} write
\[
v_t\mid\sigma\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\text{rate}=1/\sigma),
\qquad
Y_t\mid v_t,\ldots\sim
\mathcal N\{\mu_t+A_pv_t,\,\sigma B_pv_t\},
\]
so that \(\E(v_t)=\sigma\). Parts of Chapter~\ref{ch:research-b} use the
standardized variable \(z_t\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)\). The substitution
\(v_t=\sigma z_t\) changes the displayed conditional variance to
\(B_p\sigma^2z_t\). Formulas involving powers of \(\sigma\) must therefore
be read under the convention stated in the corresponding chapter. Normal
distributions are parameterized by mean and variance throughout.

\section{Multiple quantiles and predictive distributions}

Separate quantile fits, post-fit predictive synthesis, and a stacked
multiquantile update are different constructions. The \pkg{exdqlm} and
hydrologic procedures combine information after fitting level-specific
models. MQ--DESN instead reuses the observed response in a composite update
and couples adjacent coefficient vectors. None of these operations alone
defines a unique joint response distribution.

The evaluation criterion follows the declared target. Check loss evaluates a
specified conditional quantile. CRPS evaluates a predictive distribution,
whereas the Q--DESN finite-grid \(\aCRPS\) depends on its fitted probability
range and grid. Empirical response frequencies describe a particular
evaluation record; they are not posterior intervals or general calibration
theorems. Tolerance confidence concerns the repeated-sampling behavior of the
complete interval-selection rule.
