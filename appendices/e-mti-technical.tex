\section{Population and empirical results}
\label{app:mti-proofs}

Fix a conditioning value \(x\) and suppress it from the notation; the
unconditional case follows by omitting \(X\). Write
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],
\qquad
M_c(u)=\frac1c\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
For regular endpoints \(a<b\), the two population endpoint scores imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=c,
\qquad
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\}=c\mu.
\]
Equivalently, the omitted lower and upper tails balance first moments about
\(\mu\); their probabilities need not be equal.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}}

Let \(e=(Y-a)(Y-b)\) and, away from endpoint atoms, put
\(s=c-\ind(e<0)=c-\ind(a<Y<b)\). Differentiating the conditional risk is
justified by the interchange condition in the proposition. The endpoint
scores are
\[
\E\{s(b-Y)\mid X=x\}=0,
\qquad
\E\{s(a-Y)\mid X=x\}=0.
\]
Because \(a<b\), subtracting the scores gives
\(\E(s\mid X=x)=0\), which is
Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-content-score}. Substitution into either score gives
\(\E(sY\mid X=x)=0\), which is
Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-retained-mean-score}. The first identity is the
population-content result of \citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR}; the second is the
additional retained-mean consequence used here.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:quantile-window}}

Modulo probability-zero portions outside the support, every contiguous
content-\(c\) interval is \(I_c(u)\). If \(v>u\), then
\[
M_c(v)-M_c(u)
=\frac1c\int_0^c\{Q(v+s)-Q(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
The mean of the lowest \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu\), whereas that of the
highest \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu\). Continuity and strict monotonicity
therefore give a unique \(u_c\) satisfying \(M_c(u_c)=\mu\). The retained-mean
score identifies \(Q(u_c)\) and \(Q(u_c+c)\). Under symmetry, the window
beginning at \((1-c)/2\) has retained mean \(\mu\), so uniqueness gives the
equal-tailed interval.

For integrable random variables \(U\) and \(V\) with the same finite mean,
\(U\preceq_{\rm cx}V\) means
\(\E\{\varphi(U)\}\leq\E\{\varphi(V)\}\) for every convex function
\(\varphi\) for which both expectations are finite.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving contraction]
\label{rqr2026:prop:supp-convex-order}
Let \(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(Y_{\mathrm{out}}\) have the conditional laws of
\(Y\) inside and outside a regular MPI target. For every convex \(\varphi\)
with finite required expectations,
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le \E\{\varphi(Y)\}
\le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
Thus
\(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y
\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y_{\mathrm{out}}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(s_\varphi\) be the affine secant through
\(\{a,\varphi(a)\}\) and \(\{b,\varphi(b)\}\). Convexity gives
\(\varphi(y)\le s_\varphi(y)\) inside \([a,b]\) and the reverse inequality
outside. Both conditional distributions have mean \(\mu\), so
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le s_\varphi(\mu)
\le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
The law of \(Y\) is their mixture. Taking
\(\varphi(y)=(y-\mu)^2\) gives the corresponding variance ordering when
second moments exist.
\end{proof}

The regular MPI path is nested. With \(L_c=Q(u_c)\) and
\(U_c=Q(u_c+c)\), implicit differentiation gives
\[
\frac{du_c}{dc}=-\frac{U_c-\mu}{U_c-L_c}<0,
\qquad
\frac{d(u_c+c)}{dc}=\frac{\mu-L_c}{U_c-L_c}>0.
\]
Convergence of both endpoints to \(\mu\) as \(c\downarrow0\) additionally
requires local support at the mean. At endpoint atoms the directional
conditions yield only
\(\Pr(a<Y<b)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b)\). Restricted regression roots satisfy
design-weighted score equations and need not attain conditional content at
each covariate value. Positive affine response transformations preserve the
target after transforming the tilt and learning rate; arbitrary monotone
transformations do not.

\subsection{Mean tilt and profiled identification}

Away from endpoint atoms, differentiating the expected MTI loss with respect
to half-width and midpoint first gives content \(c\) and then
\(\E\{Y\ind(|Y-m|<h)\}=c(\mu+\delta)\). Strict monotonicity of
\(M_c\) therefore gives the unique interior window asserted in
Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:mean-tilt}.

\begin{lemma}[Profile radius and envelope derivative]
\label{rqr2026:lem:supp-profile-radius}
Under the assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt}, for every \(m\in\R\) there is
a unique \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\(\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c\). If
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\},
\qquad
u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\},
\]
then, at interior roots,
\begin{equation}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=2c[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta],
\label{rqr2026:eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}
\end{equation}
with the corresponding one-sided derivatives at finite support transitions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\),
\(H_m(h)=F(m+h)-F(m-h)\) is continuous, begins at zero, and converges to
one. Connected support and strict increase give a unique content-\(c\)
crossing and continuity of its inverse. At interior roots,
\(\partial H_m(h)/\partial h=f(m+h)+f(m-h)>0\), so the implicit-function
theorem applies.

For \(Z_m=(Y-m)^2\), the subgradient of
\(q\mapsto\E\{\rho_c(Z_m-q)\}\) brackets zero uniquely at
\(q=h_c(m)^2\). On compact midpoint neighborhoods the score is dominated by
an integrable multiple of \(1+|Y|\). The profiling inequalities therefore
give
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-\ind(|Y-m|<h_c(m))\}]-2c\delta,
\]
which reduces to Equation~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}.
The same argument yields one-sided derivatives at support transitions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt}}

Write \(a_c(m)=m-h_c(m)\), \(b_c(m)=m+h_c(m)\), and
\(u_c(m)=F\{a_c(m)\}\). At interior roots,
\[
m=\{Q(u_c(m))+Q(u_c(m)+c)\}/2.
\]
The right-hand side is strictly increasing in \(u\). When a root passes a
finite support endpoint, \(u_c(m)\) is clamped at zero or \(1-c\); hence it
remains globally nondecreasing. The lemma and strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
show that the profiled derivative is negative before the unique solution of
\(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) and positive afterward. This establishes the unique
global midpoint minimizer; fixed-midpoint profiling gives its unique positive
radius.

The admissible population range is
\[
M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu.
\]
Interior tilts correspond to finite-root windows. At a boundary tilt, an
infinite support endpoint gives a semi-infinite limit; a finite endpoint needs
a support constraint to identify an otherwise inactive root. Outside the
closed range, the unrestricted expected loss is unbounded below along the
corresponding escaping-root direction.

\subsection{Fractional empirical balance}

An intercept-only endpoint fit uses one pair \((a,b)\) for all observations,
unlike the covariate-dependent endpoint functions considered in the chapter.
For the unpenalized empirical MTI criterion, let
\(e_i=(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\). Its check-loss subdifferential is
\[
\partial\rho_c(e_i)=
\begin{cases}
\{c-1\},&e_i<0,\\
[c-1,c],&e_i=0,\\
\{c\},&e_i>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Choose \(s_i\in\partial\rho_c(e_i)\) jointly so that the two endpoint
subgradient equations hold, and define \(q_i=c-s_i\). The \(q_i\) are
fractional membership allocations: \(q_i=1\) strictly inside the fitted
interval, \(q_i=0\) strictly outside, and \(q_i\in[0,1]\) only when an
observation equals a fitted root.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fractional empirical balance]
\label{rqr2026:thm:supp-fractional-balance}
At an interior subgradient-stationary solution, allocations
\(q_i\in[0,1]\) can be chosen such that
\[
\sum_iq_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_iq_iy_i=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint subgradient equations are
\[
\sum_i s_i(b-y_i)-nc\delta=0,
\qquad
\sum_i s_i(a-y_i)-nc\delta=0.
\]
Their difference gives \(\sum_i s_i=0\), hence \(\sum_iq_i=nc\).
Substitution into either equation gives the retained-mean identity.
\end{proof}

If \(B_n\) observations lie at fitted roots, then
\[
P_n(a<Y<b)\le c\le P_n(a\le Y\le b),
\qquad
|P_n(a<Y<b)-c|\le B_n/n.
\]
For iid intercept-only data, Massart's DKW inequality implies, with probability
at least \(1-\alpha\),
\[
|\Pr_F(a<Y<b)-c|
\le B_n/n+\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}.
\]
This is a conservative bound, not exact future coverage or a
restricted-regression result. The width penalty in RQR-W changes both content
and placement through its endpoint scores; it is not merely a rule for
breaking ties among MPI intervals.

\section{Profile computation and content checking}
\label{app:mti-profile-computation}

For fitted content \(q\), tilt \(\delta\), and learning rate
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the intercept-only calculation uses the independent
root priors
\[
a,b\stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}\Normal(0,\lambda^{-1}),
\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
Its negative log generalized posterior, up to an additive constant, is
\[
J(a,b)=\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_i
[\rho_q\{(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\}-q\delta(a+b-2y_i)]
+\frac\lambda2(a^2+b^2).
\]
Set \(m=(a+b)/2\), \(t=(b-a)^2/4\), and
\(s=nq-\lambda/\omega_{\mathrm R}\).

\begin{proposition}[Convex midpoint profile]
\label{rqr2026:prop:supp-convex-intercept-profile}
Suppose \(0<s<n\), and let
\(W_s=\{w\in[0,1]^n:\sum_iw_i=s\}\). For fixed \(m\), a minimizing
\(t\ge0\) is an empirical \(s/n\) quantile of the observed squared distances
\((y_i-m)^2\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\). Moreover,
\begin{align*}
\min_{t\ge0}J(m,t)
&=2\lambda m^2+\max_{w\in W_s}\{A(w)+B(w)m\},\\
A(w)&=\omega_{\mathrm R}
[q\textstyle\sum_i y_i^2-\sum_iw_i y_i^2+2q\delta\sum_i y_i],\\
B(w)&=2\omega_{\mathrm R}
[\textstyle\sum_iw_i y_i-q\sum_i y_i-nq\delta].
\end{align*}
The profile is strongly convex in \(m\); the minimizing squared half-width
can be nonunique at an empirical quantile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(z_i=(y_i-m)^2\), write
\(\rho_q(z_i-t)=qz_i-qt+(t-z_i)_+\). Then
\[
\min_{t\ge0}\{\textstyle\sum_i(t-z_i)_+-st\}
=-\min_{w\in W_s}\textstyle\sum_iw_iz_i.
\]
Assigning unit weight to the smallest \(\lfloor s\rfloor\) distances and the
remaining mass to the next distance establishes the quantile statement.
Expanding the squared distances gives the displayed profile. A maximum of
affine functions is convex, and \(2\lambda m^2\) makes it strongly convex.
\end{proof}

The calculation minimizes this scalar profile, uses the lower squared radius
at a tie, and checks the final objective against a convex lower bound with
relative tolerance \(10^{-8}\). The reported study uses
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}=1\), \(\lambda=10^{-4}\), and zero prior means.

For a fixed interval \(I\), direct Dirichlet-process conjugacy gives
\[
F(I)\mid D_n\sim
\operatorname{Beta}\{aH(I)+N_n(I),\,a(1-H(I))+n-N_n(I)\}.
\]
The fitted procedure uses \(a=1\) and \(H=\Normal(0,4^2)\) on the fitting
scale. The resulting survival probability is a posterior content statement
under this response-distribution model, not a distribution-free tolerance
guarantee.

\section{Calibration and sample-range reference}
\label{app:mti-calibration}

The initial content menu adds the offsets
\[
\mathcal O_q=\{-.04,-.02,-.01,-.005,-.0025,-.001,0,.001,.0025,.005,.01\}
\]
to the scan-reference fraction, retains values above \(c+10^{-4}\), and
truncates at 0.9999. When the reference is at least 0.990 or uses the full
sample range, it also includes
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal B_q=\{&.995,.996,.9965,.997,.99725,.9975,.998,\\
&.9985,.999,.99925,.9995,.99975,.9999\}.
\end{split}
\]
The effective-mass rule in Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d} then
adds one midpoint for each missing rank interval. The tilt menu includes zero,
the fractional shortest-window offset, and seven equally spaced values over
the empirical admissible range.

For each of the nine \((n,c)\) settings and eight laws in
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d}, 32 independent development samples per law define
an initial menu: 25 empirical quantiles of candidate content probabilities and
the requested confidence, for 26 cutoffs. An independent assessment then uses
\(R=24{,}000\) samples per law and cutoff. If \(S\) runs both produce an
interval and attain content \(c\), the one-sided exact binomial lower bound is
\[
L_{\eta}(S,R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&S=0,\\
\operatorname{Beta}^{-1}(\eta;S,R-S+1),&S>0.
\end{cases}
\]
A cutoff is accepted only if all \(R\) intervals are produced and
\(L_\eta(S,R)\ge0.95\) for every law; the smallest accepted cutoff is retained.

The initial allocation is \(\eta=0.025/(9\cdot8\cdot26)\). Seven settings
pass. A prespecified follow-up uses fresh development and assessment samples
for \((n,c)=(50,0.90)\) and \((100,0.95)\), with 105 cutoffs per setting; the
other seven cutoffs remain unchanged. Its allocation is
\(\eta=0.025/(2\cdot8\cdot105)\). Splitting the total error probability 0.05
between stages and applying the union bound over settings, laws, and assessed
cutoffs gives the simultaneous calibration statement.

The cutoff is fixed before 1,000 independent validation samples are generated
for every law and setting. Of the 72 simultaneous validation intervals, 38 have
lower bounds above 0.95, 34 include 0.95, and none has an upper bound below
0.95. The smallest point estimate is 951/1000. These results validate the
prespecified finite panel empirically; they do not establish a
distribution-free MTI tolerance theorem.

For a sample-bounded two-sided order-statistic interval, the full-range
feasibility probability is
\[
\Pr_F^n\{F(Y_{(n)})-F(Y_{(1)})\ge c\}

\section{Proposed endpoint extensions}
\label{app:mti-extensions}

For raw endpoint functions \(\eta_{1i}\) and \(\eta_{2i}\), put
\(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\). The product-residual check factor has
the normal--exponential representation
\begin{align*}
&\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\exp\{-\rho_c(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal(e_i\mid\xi_cv_i,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)
\Exp(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i,
\end{align*}
where \(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\),
\(\xi_c=(1-2c)/\{c(1-c)\}\), and
\(\phi_c=2/\{c(1-c)\}\). This augments the loss update; it is not a response
model.

For linear roots \(\eta_j=X\beta_j\), condition on the second root and define
\[
A_1=\diag(y-\eta_2)X,
\qquad
z_1=y\circ(y-\eta_2)-\xi_cv,
\qquad
W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}\}.
\]
With Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((Q_{01},h_{01})\),
\[
Q_1=Q_{01}+A_1^\top W_vA_1,
\qquad
h_1=h_{01}+A_1^\top W_vz_1
+\omega_{\mathrm R}cX^\top\delta,
\]
and \(\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim\Normal(Q_1^{-1}h_1,Q_1^{-1})\). The other-root
update exchanges labels. Reported endpoints are sorted at each design point,
so coefficient labels are not interpreted as globally lower or upper roots.
Proper Gaussian priors make the finite-dimensional update normalizable; a
nonzero tilt with heavier-tailed random scales needs a separate propriety
argument.

For time-varying endpoints,
\[
\eta_{jt}=f_t^\top\theta_{jt},
\qquad
\theta_{jt}=G_t\theta_{j,t-1}+\varepsilon_{jt},
\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Conditional on one complete root path and the latent scales, the other path is
linear Gaussian and can be updated by forward filtering and backward sampling.
These regression and dynamic calculations remain proposals: they have no
matched implementation, simulation study, predictive assessment, or tolerance
calibration.
