\section{Population and empirical results}
\label{app:mti-proofs}

Fix a conditioning value \(x\) and suppress it from the notation; the
unconditional case follows by omitting \(X\). Write
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],
\qquad
M_c(u)=\frac1c\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
For regular endpoints \(a<b\), the two population endpoint scores imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=c,
\qquad
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\}=c\mu.
\]
Equivalently, the omitted lower and upper tails balance first moments about
\(\mu\); their probabilities need not be equal.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}}

Let \(e=(Y-a)(Y-b)\) and, away from endpoint atoms, put
\(s=c-\ind(e<0)=c-\ind(a<Y<b)\). Differentiating the conditional risk is
justified by the interchange condition in the proposition. The endpoint
scores are
\[
\E\{s(b-Y)\mid X=x\}=0,
\qquad
\E\{s(a-Y)\mid X=x\}=0.
\]
Because \(a<b\), subtracting the scores gives
\(\E(s\mid X=x)=0\), which is
Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-content-score}. Substitution into either score gives
\(\E(sY\mid X=x)=0\), which is
Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-retained-mean-score}. The first identity is the
population-content result of \citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR}; the second is the
additional retained-mean consequence used here.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:quantile-window}}

Modulo probability-zero portions outside the support, every contiguous
content-\(c\) interval is \(I_c(u)\). If \(v>u\), then
\[
M_c(v)-M_c(u)
=\frac1c\int_0^c\{Q(v+s)-Q(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
The mean of the lowest \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu\), whereas that of the
highest \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu\). Continuity and strict monotonicity
therefore give a unique \(u_c\) satisfying \(M_c(u_c)=\mu\). The retained-mean
score identifies \(Q(u_c)\) and \(Q(u_c+c)\). Under symmetry, the window
beginning at \((1-c)/2\) has retained mean \(\mu\), so uniqueness gives the
equal-tailed interval.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt}}

Away from endpoint atoms, differentiating the expected MTI loss with respect
to half-width and midpoint gives content \(c\) and retained mean
\(\mu+\delta\). For a fixed midpoint \(m\),
\(H_m(h)=F(m+h)-F(m-h)\) is continuous, begins at zero, and converges to one.
Connected support and strict increase therefore give a unique positive radius
\(h_c(m)\) satisfying \(H_m\{h_c(m)\}=c\).

Define
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\},
\qquad
u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}.
\]
At interior roots, the implicit-function theorem applies because
\(f(m+h)+f(m-h)>0\). Profiling the squared radius and differentiating the
midpoint criterion gives
\begin{equation}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=2c[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta].
\label{rqr2026:eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}
\end{equation}
The finite second moment supplies an integrable envelope; the corresponding
one-sided derivatives apply at finite support transitions.

Write \(a_c(m)=m-h_c(m)\), \(b_c(m)=m+h_c(m)\), and
\(u_c(m)=F\{a_c(m)\}\). At interior roots,
\[
m=\{Q(u_c(m))+Q(u_c(m)+c)\}/2.
\]
The right-hand side is strictly increasing in \(u\). When a root passes a
finite support endpoint, \(u_c(m)\) is clamped at zero or \(1-c\); hence it
remains globally nondecreasing. Equation
\eqref{rqr2026:eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative} and strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
show that the profiled derivative is negative before the unique solution of
\(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) and positive afterward. This establishes the unique
global midpoint minimizer; fixed-midpoint profiling gives its unique positive
radius.

The admissible population range is
\[
M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu.
\]
Interior tilts correspond to finite-root windows. At a boundary tilt, an
infinite support endpoint gives a semi-infinite limit; a finite endpoint needs
a support constraint to identify an otherwise inactive root. Outside the
closed range, the unrestricted expected loss is unbounded below along the
corresponding escaping-root direction.

\subsection{Fractional empirical balance}

An intercept-only endpoint fit uses one pair \((a,b)\) for all observations,
unlike the covariate-dependent endpoint functions considered in the chapter.
For the unpenalized empirical MTI criterion, let
\(e_i=(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\). Its check-loss subdifferential is
\[
\partial\rho_c(e_i)=
\begin{cases}
\{c-1\},&e_i<0,\\
[c-1,c],&e_i=0,\\
\{c\},&e_i>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Choose \(s_i\in\partial\rho_c(e_i)\) jointly so that the two endpoint
subgradient equations hold, and define \(q_i=c-s_i\). The \(q_i\) are
fractional membership allocations: \(q_i=1\) strictly inside the fitted
interval, \(q_i=0\) strictly outside, and \(q_i\in[0,1]\) only when an
observation equals a fitted root.

At an interior subgradient-stationary solution, the allocations can be chosen
to satisfy
\[
\sum_iq_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_iq_iy_i=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
The endpoint subgradient equations are
\[
\sum_i s_i(b-y_i)-nc\delta=0,
\qquad
\sum_i s_i(a-y_i)-nc\delta=0.
\]
Their difference gives \(\sum_i s_i=0\), hence \(\sum_iq_i=nc\).
Substitution into either equation gives the retained-mean identity.
These identities explain the empirical balance reported in
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d}; they are estimating equations for the unpenalized
intercept-only fit, not tolerance guarantees.

\section{Fixed-target computation and content checking}
\label{app:mti-computation}

For fitted content \(q\), tilt \(\delta\), and learning rate
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the intercept-only calculation uses the independent
root priors
\[
a,b\stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}\Normal(0,\lambda^{-1}),
\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
Its negative log generalized posterior, up to an additive constant, is
\[
J(a,b)=\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_i
[\rho_q\{(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\}-q\delta(a+b-2y_i)]
+\frac\lambda2(a^2+b^2).
\]
For every finite sample and fixed finite tilt, this generalized posterior is
proper. The check loss is nonnegative, so its exponential is bounded above
by one. The remaining linear tilt has a finite expectation under the
Gaussian prior: for \(t=\omega_{\mathrm R}nq\delta\),
\(E_{\pi_0}\{\exp[t(a+b)]\}=\exp(t^2/\lambda)\). Completing the square
also bounds all polynomial root moments. This argument uses the stated
proper Gaussian priors; random heavy-tailed scales require a separate
argument for nonzero tilt.

\subsection{Augmentation and Gibbs sampling}

With the constants defined in Section~\ref{rqr2026:sec:static-theory-statements},
the product-residual factor satisfies
\begin{align*}
&\frac{q(1-q)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
  e^{-\rho_q(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}}\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal(e_i\mid\xi_qv_i,\phi_q\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)
\Exp(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i.
\end{align*}
The multiplier on the left is independent of the roots because
\(q\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) are fixed. The augmented kernel is therefore
\begin{align*}
\pi(a,b,v\mid D_n)\propto{}&
\pi_0(a)\pi_0(b)e^{\omega_{\mathrm R}nq\delta(a+b)}\\
&\times\prod_i v_i^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(e_i-\xi_qv_i)^2}
{2\phi_q\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i}-\frac{v_i}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}\right].
\end{align*}
Terms depending only on the sample and fixed settings are absorbed into the
constant. This augments an endpoint loss; it does not transform a response
sampling density.

Expanding the quadratic in one root gives the Gaussian canonical parameters
in Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mti-root-conditionals}. Collecting the powers of
\(v_i\) and its reciprocal gives the GIG conditional in
Eq.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mti-scale-conditional}. At \(e_i=0\), this distribution
is Gamma with shape \(1/2\) and rate
\(1/\{4\sigma_{\mathrm R}q(1-q)\}\). Its inverse moment is infinite,
although the conditional distribution itself is proper.

\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmblock}{Fixed-target MTI Gibbs sampling}
\label{alg:ch05-mti-gibbs}
Fix \(q,\delta,\omega_{\mathrm R},\lambda\) and choose dispersed initial
raw roots for independent chains.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw each latent scale from its GIG conditional, using the Gamma limit
at a zero product residual.
\item Draw \(a\) from its Gaussian conditional, then draw \(b\) conditional
on the updated \(a\) and the same latent scales.
\item After burn-in, sort each retained root pair to obtain lower and upper
endpoints and their width. Retain chain identities and objective values.
\end{enumerate}
Assess mixing and Monte Carlo error for these label-invariant summaries.
\end{algorithmblock}
\end{minipage}\par

The sampler describes uncertainty conditional on the specified content and
tilt. A content--tilt selection rule is a separate operation. Posterior
endpoint means, medians, and modes are different summaries, and their
intervals need not have the same content or width.

\subsection{ECM calculation and empirical balance}

For \(e_i\ne0\), the E-step uses
\[
\tau_i=E(v_i^{-1}\mid\cdot)=\frac{1}{q(1-q)|e_i|}.
\]
Let \(d_i=y_i-b\). Holding the second root fixed, conditional maximization
gives
\[
a^+=\frac{\sum_i d_i(\tau_i y_i d_i-\xi_q)/
 (\phi_q\sigma_{\mathrm R})+\omega_{\mathrm R}nq\delta}
 {\lambda+\sum_i\tau_i d_i^2/(\phi_q\sigma_{\mathrm R})}.
\]
The second maximization exchanges root labels and uses the updated
\(a^+\), while retaining the same E-step weights. The calculation uses a
residual-product floor near zero and checks the original objective \(J\),
with backtracking when necessary.

\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmblock}{Fixed-target MTI--ECM calculation}
\label{alg:ch05-mti-ecm}
Fix \(q,\delta,\omega_{\mathrm R},\lambda\) and deterministic starting roots.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute the product residuals and inverse-scale moments, using the
prespecified floor near zero.
\item Update the first root and then the second root by conditional
maximization, using one set of E-step weights.
\item Accept the cycle if \(J\) does not increase beyond the numerical
allowance; otherwise backtrack toward the preceding roots.
\item Stop at the stated criterion or iteration limit. Return the final
sorted roots, objective, stopping flag, and stationarity diagnostic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmblock}
\end{minipage}\par

Each candidate starts from empirical fractional shortest-window endpoints
shifted according to its tilt. The reported computation uses one start,
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}=1\), \(\lambda=10^{-4}\), at most 100 cycles,
stationarity tolerance \(10^{-3}\), and a residual-product floor of
\(10^{-8}\) times the implementation's response-product scale. The floor
makes the update approximate near zero residuals.

At a distinct-root stationary point, choose joint subgradients
\(s_i\in\partial\rho_q(e_i)\) satisfying the penalized endpoint equations.
With \(u_i=q-s_i\), subtraction and substitution give
\[
\sum_i u_i=nq-\lambda/\omega_{\mathrm R},\qquad
\sum_i u_i y_i=nq(\overline y+\delta)
-\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{\mathrm R}}(a+b).
\]
These identities show the prior contributions to empirical balance; finite
iterates need not satisfy them exactly. In the simulation, 42,116 of
72,000 selected fits meet the stopping criterion and 29,884 reach the cap.
All selected fits reach the cap at \((n,c)=(50,.90),(100,.95),(500,.99)\).
In the application, the corresponding counts are 90 of 1,000 primary
tensile fits and 14 of 1,000 primary RSD fits; across all partitions and
both responses, 170 of 3,004 pass. Every returned interval remains in the
empirical comparison. These stopping diagnostics do not certify global
mode estimation.

\subsection{Posterior content assessment}

For a fixed interval \(I\), direct Dirichlet-process conjugacy gives
\[
F(I)\mid D_n\sim
\operatorname{Beta}\{\alpha_0H(I)+N_n(I),\,
\alpha_0(1-H(I))+n-N_n(I)\}.
\]
The fitted procedure uses \(\alpha_0=1\) and \(H=\Normal(0,4^2)\) on the fitting
scale. The resulting survival probability is a posterior content statement
under this response-distribution model; repeated-sampling simulation evaluates
the complete tolerance rule.

\section{Calibration and sample-range reference}
\label{app:mti-calibration}

The initial content menu adds the offsets
\[
\mathcal O_q=\{-.04,-.02,-.01,-.005,-.0025,-.001,0,.001,.0025,.005,.01\}
\]
to the scan-reference fraction, retains values above \(c+10^{-4}\), and
truncates at 0.9999. When the reference is at least 0.990 or uses the full
sample range, it also includes
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal B_q=\{&.995,.996,.9965,.997,.99725,.9975,.998,\\
&.9985,.999,.99925,.9995,.99975,.9999\}.
\end{split}
\]
At each content, the tilt menu adds
\(-.45,-.30,-.15,0,.15,.30,.45\) sample standard deviations to the
empirical fractional shortest-window tilt and includes zero. Candidates
whose absolute tilt exceeds two sample standard deviations are removed.
The fixed construction also allows a deterministic expansion when the
initial set has no eligible candidate; this is not replacement by a
different interval method.

For each of the nine \((n,c)\) settings and eight distributions in
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d}, the calibration assesses candidate cutoffs on
\(R=1000\) samples per distribution. If \(S\) runs both produce an
interval and attain content \(c\), the one-sided exact binomial lower bound is
\[
L_{\eta}(S,R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&S=0,\\
\operatorname{Beta}^{-1}(\eta;S,R-S+1),&S>0.
\end{cases}
\]
These are pointwise lower bounds. Selection considers content attainment,
absence of observed nonreturns, and width stability. The fixed cutoffs are
\(0.995\), except for \(0.996\) at \(n=1000,c=0.99\).
The smallest distribution-specific lower bounds are approximately
\(0.9460\) at \(n=100,c=0.95\) and \(0.9382\) at \(n=500,c=0.99\);
the other seven setting minima exceed \(0.95\). They do not adjust for the
cutoff search or establish a simultaneous guarantee for the selected rule.

The cutoff is fixed before 1,000 independent validation samples are generated
for every distribution and setting. Simultaneous two-sided intervals allocate
\(0.05/(2\cdot72)\) to each tail. Of the 72 validation intervals, 53 have
lower bounds above 0.95, 19 include 0.95, and none has an upper bound below
0.95. The smallest point estimate is 951/1000. These results characterize the
prespecified rule over the eight-distribution panel.

For a sample-bounded two-sided order-statistic interval, the full-range
feasibility probability is
\[
\Pr_F^n\{F(Y_{(n)})-F(Y_{(1)})\ge c\}
=1-n(1-c)c^{n-1}-c^n.
\]
The reported design excludes settings that cannot support the requested content
and 0.95 tolerance confidence under this reference calculation.

\section{Proposed endpoint extensions}
\label{app:mti-extensions}

For raw endpoint functions \(\eta_{1i}\) and \(\eta_{2i}\), put
\(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\). The product-residual check factor has
the normal--exponential representation
\begin{align*}
&\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\exp\{-\rho_c(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal(e_i\mid\xi_cv_i,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)
\Exp(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i,
\end{align*}
where \(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\),
\(\xi_c=(1-2c)/\{c(1-c)\}\), and
\(\phi_c=2/\{c(1-c)\}\). This representation provides a latent-variable
augmentation for the loss update.

For linear roots \(\eta_j=X\beta_j\), condition on the second root and define
\[
A_1=\diag(y-\eta_2)X,
\qquad
z_1=y\circ(y-\eta_2)-\xi_cv,
\qquad
W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}\}.
\]
With Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((Q_{01},h_{01})\),
\[
Q_1=Q_{01}+A_1^\top W_vA_1,
\qquad
h_1=h_{01}+A_1^\top W_vz_1
+\omega_{\mathrm R}cX^\top\delta,
\]
and \(\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim\Normal(Q_1^{-1}h_1,Q_1^{-1})\). The other-root
update exchanges labels. Reported endpoints are sorted at each design point,
so coefficient labels are not interpreted as globally lower or upper roots.
Proper Gaussian priors make the finite-dimensional update normalizable; a
nonzero tilt with heavier-tailed random scales needs a separate propriety
argument.

For time-varying endpoints,
\[
\eta_{jt}=f_t^\top\theta_{jt},
\qquad
\theta_{jt}=G_t\theta_{j,t-1}+\varepsilon_{jt},
\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Conditional on one complete root path and the latent scales, the other path is
linear Gaussian and can be updated by forward filtering and backward sampling.
Simulation studies, predictive assessment, and tolerance calibration for these
regression and dynamic extensions remain future work.
