\section{Population and empirical results}
\label{app:mti-proofs}

\subsection{Endpoint scores and the quantile-window target}

Suppress the conditioning variable and write
\[
 I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad
 M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,
 \qquad 0\le u\le 1-c.
\]
For endpoints \(a<b\), let \(I_{a,b}(Y)=\ind(a<Y<b)\). Away from endpoint
atoms, the derivative of the MPI check loss with respect to its scalar product
residual is \(c-I_{a,b}(Y)\). Under the differentiability conditions of
Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}, the endpoint score
equations are
\begin{align}
 \E[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(b-Y)]&=0,\label{rqr2026:eq:pop-foc-a}\\
 \E[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(a-Y)]&=0.\label{rqr2026:eq:pop-foc-b}
\end{align}
Their difference gives \(\Pr(a<Y<b)=c\), and substitution gives
\(\E\{YI_{a,b}(Y)\}=c\mu\). Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \E[\{\mu-Y\}\ind(Y<a)]
 =\E[\{Y-\mu\}\ind(Y>b)].
 \label{rqr2026:eq:rqr-tail-moment-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus the omitted tails need not have equal probabilities; their first moments
about the mean balance.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:quantile-window}}

Modulo probability-zero portions outside the support, every contiguous
interval having content \(c\) has the quantile-window form \(I_c(u)\). If
\(v>u\), then
\[
 M_c(v)-M_c(u)
 =\frac{1}{c}\int_0^c\{Q(v+s)-Q(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
The average of the lowest \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu\), and the average of
the highest \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu\), for a nondegenerate distribution
with finite mean. Continuity and strict monotonicity therefore give existence
and uniqueness of \(u_c\) satisfying \(M_c(u_c)=\mu\). The first-moment score
equation identifies the endpoints \(Q(u_c)\) and \(Q(u_c+c)\). If the law is
symmetric about \(\mu\), the window beginning at \((1-c)/2\) has retained mean
\(\mu\), so uniqueness gives the equal-tailed interval.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving contraction]
\label{rqr2026:prop:supp-convex-order}
Let \(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(Y_{\mathrm{out}}\) have the conditional
distributions of \(Y\) given \(a<Y<b\) and \(Y\notin(a,b)\), respectively,
at a regular MPI target. For every convex function \(\varphi\) for which the
required expectations exist,
\[
 \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
 \le \E\{\varphi(Y)\}
 \le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
Consequently,
\(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y
\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y_{\mathrm{out}}\). If second moments exist, the same
ordering holds for the corresponding variances.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(s_\varphi\) be the affine secant through
\(\{a,\varphi(a)\}\) and \(\{b,\varphi(b)\}\). Convexity gives
\(\varphi(y)\le s_\varphi(y)\) on \([a,b]\) and
\(\varphi(y)\ge s_\varphi(y)\) outside that interval. Both conditional
distributions have mean \(\mu\), so
\[
 \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
 \le s_\varphi(\mu)
 \le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
The full distribution is their mixture with weights \(c\) and \(1-c\), which
places its convex expectation between the two. Taking
\(\varphi(y)=(y-\mu)^2\) gives the variance statement.
\end{proof}

The regular MPI path is nested. With \(L_c=Q(u_c)\) and
\(U_c=Q(u_c+c)\), differentiation of
\(\int_{u_c}^{u_c+c}\{Q(v)-\mu\}\,dv=0\) gives
\[
 \frac{du_c}{dc}=-\frac{U_c-\mu}{U_c-L_c}<0,
 \qquad
 \frac{d(u_c+c)}{dc}=\frac{\mu-L_c}{U_c-L_c}>0.
\]
The limit \(L_c,U_c\to\mu\) as \(c\downarrow0\) additionally requires local
support at the mean. For example, a symmetric law supported on
\([-2,-1]\cup[1,2]\) has an MPI window that straddles the gap even as its
content tends to zero. Under a positive affine transformation
\(Y^\star=r+sY\), \(s>0\), the endpoints transform affinely, the tilt becomes
\(s\delta\), and a generalized-Bayes learning rate becomes
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}/s^2\). Nonlinear monotone transformations generally
change the target.

At endpoint atoms, directional half-width derivatives give only
\[
 \Pr(a<Y<b)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b).
\]
Exact retained-mean equality then requires a joint subgradient allocation.
For restricted regression roots, the score equations are design-weighted
projections and need not imply conditional coverage at each covariate value.

\subsection{Mean tilt and profiled identification}

For fixed response-scale tilt \(\delta\), the MTI loss is
\begin{align}
 \ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(m,h;y)
 &=\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y),
 \label{rqr2026:eq:mean-tilted-loss-midpoint}\\
 \ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(a,b;y)
 &=\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
 \label{rqr2026:eq:mean-tilted-loss-roots}
\end{align}
The tilt changes placement but not the content score.

\subsubsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:mean-tilt}}

Let \(I_{m,h}(Y)=\ind(|Y-m|<h)\). Away from endpoint atoms,
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\partial}{\partial h}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
 &=2h\{\Pr(I_{m,h}=1)-c\},\\
 \frac{\partial}{\partial m}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
 &=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-I_{m,h}(Y)\}]-2c\delta.
\end{align*}
The first equation gives content \(c\). Substitution into the second gives
\(\E\{YI_{m,h}(Y)\}=c(\mu+\delta)\). Strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
then gives the unique interior quantile-window index.

\begin{lemma}[Profile radius and envelope derivative]
\label{rqr2026:lem:supp-profile-radius}
Suppose the support closure is an interval, \(F\) is absolutely continuous
and strictly increasing between its support endpoints, its density is positive
and continuous on the support interior, and \(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\)
by zero and one beyond finite support endpoints. For every \(m\in\R\), there
is a unique \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\(\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c\). The radius is continuous in \(m\) and
differentiable when both roots are interior. If
\[
 \overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
 =\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\},
 \qquad u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\},
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
 =2c[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta],
 \label{rqr2026:eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}
\end{equation}
with corresponding one-sided derivatives when a root crosses a finite support
endpoint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\),
\(H_m(h)=F(m+h)-F(m-h)\) is continuous, begins at zero, and converges to
one. Interval support and strict increase imply a unique crossing of \(c\).
Continuity of the inverse follows from the same strict-crossing argument. When
both roots are interior, the implicit-function theorem applies because
\[
 \frac{\partial H_m(h)}{\partial h}=f(m+h)+f(m-h)>0.
\]
For fixed \(m\), put \(Z_m=(Y-m)^2\) and \(q=h^2\). The subgradient interval
of \(q\mapsto\E\{\rho_c(Z_m-q)\}\) is
\[
 [\Pr(Z_m<q)-c,\ \Pr(Z_m\le q)-c],
\]
so continuity makes \(q=h_c(m)^2\) its unique minimizer. On compact
neighborhoods of \(m\), the midpoint score is dominated by an integrable
multiple of \(1+|Y|\). The profiling inequalities and continuity of \(h_c\)
therefore give
\begin{align*}
 \overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
 &=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-I_{m,h_c(m)}(Y)\}]-2c\delta\\
 &=2c[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta].
\end{align*}
The same argument gives the one-sided derivatives at support transitions.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt}}

Write \(a_c(m)=m-h_c(m)\), \(b_c(m)=m+h_c(m)\), and
\(u_c(m)=F\{a_c(m)\}\). For \(0<u_c(m)<1-c\),
\[
 m=\frac{Q\{u_c(m)\}+Q\{u_c(m)+c\}}{2}=:m_c\{u_c(m)\}.
\]
The map \(m_c(u)\) is strictly increasing. When a root extends beyond a finite
support endpoint, \(u_c(m)\) is clamped at zero or \(1-c\); hence \(u_c\) is
globally nondecreasing. Lemma~\ref{rqr2026:lem:supp-profile-radius} and strict
monotonicity of \(M_c\) show that the profiled derivative is negative before
the unique solution of \(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) and positive afterward. The
profile therefore has a unique global midpoint minimizer, and the fixed-\(m\)
argument gives its unique positive radius.

The admissible population range is
\[
 M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu.
\]
Interior tilts correspond one-to-one with finite-root quantile windows. At a
boundary tilt, an infinite support endpoint yields a semi-infinite limiting
interval; a finite endpoint requires a support constraint to select an
otherwise inactive root. Outside the closed range, the unrestricted risk is
unbounded below along the corresponding escaping-root direction.

The recovery tilts for MPI and the equal-tailed interval are
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{MPI}}=0,
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu.
\]
For the shortest contiguous windows
\(S_{\mathrm{SH}}=\argmin_u\{Q(u+c)-Q(u)\}\), the recovery-tilt set is
\(\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\}\). These are population identities;
estimating a recovery tilt requires a separate selection rule.

For a standardized near-Normal family with small skewness \(\gamma_1\), the
first-order Cornish--Fisher calculation gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
 \approx-\frac{\gamma_1}{3}K_c,
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
 \approx-\gamma_1K_c,
 \qquad
 K_c=\frac{q_c\phi(q_c)}{c},
 \quad q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},
\]
where \(d=\delta/\sigma\). The approximation requires a quantile expansion
with a controlled remainder; small skewness alone does not control higher
cumulants or support-boundary behavior. It is therefore an initialization or
population comparison, not a finite-sample selection guarantee.

\subsection{Fractional empirical balance}

For an intercept-only fit, let
\(e_i=(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\) and choose
\(s_i\in\partial\rho_c(e_i)\). Put \(q_i=c-s_i\), so \(q_i=1\)
strictly inside the interval, \(q_i=0\) strictly outside, and
\(q_i\in[0,1]\) only at a fitted root.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fractional empirical balance]
\label{rqr2026:thm:supp-fractional-balance}
At an interior subgradient-stationary intercept-only MTI solution, allocations
\(q_i\in[0,1]\) can be chosen such that
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^nq_i=nc,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^nq_iy_i=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint subgradient equations are
\[
 \sum_i s_i(b-y_i)-nc\delta=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_i s_i(a-y_i)-nc\delta=0.
\]
Their difference gives \(\sum_i s_i=0\), hence \(\sum_iq_i=nc\).
Substitution into either endpoint equation gives the retained-mean identity.
\end{proof}

If \(B_n\) observations lie at fitted roots, then
\[
 P_n(a<Y<b)\le c\le P_n(a\le Y\le b),
 \qquad |P_n(a<Y<b)-c|\le B_n/n.
\]
For iid intercept-only data, Massart's DKW inequality implies, with probability
at least \(1-\alpha\),
\[
 |\Pr_F(a<Y<b)-c|
 \le \frac{B_n}{n}+\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\alpha)}{n}}.
\]
This is a conservative high-probability bound, not exact future coverage or a
restricted-regression result.

The width-regularized RQR-W objective of
\citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR} adds
\(\lambda_W(b-a)^2/2\) to a product-residual check loss at level \(q\).
Its interior score equations give
\[
 \Pr(a<Y<b)=q-2\lambda_W,
 \qquad
 \E(Y\mid a<Y<b)
 =\mu+\frac{2\lambda_W}{c}(\mu-m),
\]
after setting \(q=c+2\lambda_W\). Thus the penalty generally changes both
content and placement; it is not merely a tie-breaker among MPI intervals.

\section{Profile computation and content checking}
\label{app:mti-profile-computation}

For a fixed fitted content \(q\), tilt \(\delta\), learning rate
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), and independent zero-mean Gaussian root priors with
precision \(\lambda>0\), the intercept-only criterion is
\[
 J(a,b)=\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_i
 [\rho_q\{(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\}-q\delta(a+b-2y_i)]
 +\frac{\lambda}{2}(a^2+b^2).
\]
Write \(m=(a+b)/2\), \(t=(b-a)^2/4\), and
\(s=nq-\lambda/\omega_{\mathrm R}\).

\begin{proposition}[Convex midpoint profile]
\label{rqr2026:prop:supp-convex-intercept-profile}
Suppose \(0<s<n\), and let
\(W_s=\{w\in[0,1]^n:\sum_iw_i=s\}\). For fixed \(m\), a minimizing
\(t\ge0\) is an empirical \(s/n\) quantile of the squared distances
\((y_i-m)^2\). Moreover,
\begin{align*}
 \min_{t\ge0}J(m,t)
 &=2\lambda m^2+\max_{w\in W_s}\{A(w)+B(w)m\},\\
 A(w)&=\omega_{\mathrm R}
 [q\textstyle\sum_i y_i^2-\sum_iw_i y_i^2+2q\delta\sum_i y_i],\\
 B(w)&=2\omega_{\mathrm R}
 [\textstyle\sum_iw_i y_i-q\sum_i y_i-nq\delta].
\end{align*}
The profile is strongly convex in \(m\), so its minimizing midpoint is unique.
The minimizing squared half-width can be nonunique at an empirical quantile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(z_i=(y_i-m)^2\), write
\(\rho_q(z_i-t)=qz_i-qt+(t-z_i)_+\). Then
\[
 \min_{t\ge0}\{\textstyle\sum_i(t-z_i)_+-st\}
 =-\min_{w\in W_s}\textstyle\sum_iw_iz_i.
\]
The identity follows by assigning unit weight to the smallest
\(\lfloor s\rfloor\) distances and the remaining mass to the next distance;
the one-sided derivatives in \(t\) bracket zero at that quantile. Expanding
the squared distances gives the displayed profile because
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}(nq-s)=\lambda\). A maximum of affine functions is
convex, and \(2\lambda m^2\) makes the profile strongly convex.
\end{proof}

The calculation minimizes this scalar profile, chooses the lower squared
radius at a tie, and checks the final objective against a convex lower bound
with relative tolerance \(10^{-8}\). The reported validation and application
use \(\omega_{\mathrm R}=1\), \(\lambda=10^{-4}\), and zero prior means.
The effective mass \(s\) also determines the content-grid refinement. These
settings are fixed before interval selection.

For a fixed closed interval \(I\), a direct Dirichlet-process model
\(F\sim\mathrm{DP}(a,H)\) gives, by conjugacy,
\[
 F(I)\mid D_n\sim
 \mathrm{Beta}\{aH(I)+N_n(I),a(1-H(I))+n-N_n(I)\}.
\]
The analysis uses \(a=1\) and \(H=\Normal(0,4^2)\) on the fitting scale. The
Beta survival probability determines candidate eligibility. It is a posterior
content statement under this response-distribution model, not a
distribution-free tolerance guarantee.

\section{Calibration and feasibility}
\label{app:mti-calibration}

The tolerance study uses iid continuous samples, paired method comparisons,
and the nine feasible cells
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n,c)\in\{&
(50,.90),(100,.90),(100,.95),\\
&(500,.90),(500,.95),(500,.99),\\
&(1000,.90),(1000,.95),(1000,.99)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The panel consists of Gaussian and Student \(t_3\) references and six
asymmetric laws: exponential, asymmetric Laplace with quantile level \(0.10\),
two-piece Normal with scale ratio \(1{:}12\), Beta\((1,8)\),
Gamma\((0.5,1)\), and log-normal with log-scale standard deviation \(1.25\).
Each law is centered and scaled before sampling.

For each cell, independent development samples define a finite cutoff menu.
Every cutoff is assessed on 24,000 new samples from each distribution. The
selected cutoff is the smallest one that returns every interval and whose
simultaneous one-sided exact binomial lower bound for content attainment is at
least \(0.95\) for every distribution. Seven cells pass the initial
assessment. A prespecified follow-up uses fresh development and assessment
samples for \((50,.90)\) and \((100,.95)\), while retaining the seven earlier
cutoffs. Half of the total Monte Carlo error probability \(0.05\) is allocated
over the initial nine cells, eight distributions, and 26 cutoffs; half is
allocated over the two follow-up cells, eight distributions, and their
independently determined cutoff menus. The union bound controls the combined
calibration error for this finite panel.

The cutoff is fixed before 1,000 independent validation samples per
distribution and cell are generated. Of the 72 simultaneous validation

The fitted-content search begins from a scan-calibrated retained count. For

\section{Pharmaceutical application sensitivities}
\label{app:mti-application-sensitivity}

The application uses the public manufacturing records of
\citet{rqr-ZagarMihelic2022PharmaManufacturing} and the associated laboratory
file \citep{rqr-ZagarMihelic2021LaboratoryCsv}. The reported tensile metric is
reproducible from batch summaries when \(3.14\) is used in place of \(\pi\),
but its physical unit and measurement window remain unresolved. Some recorded
average hardness values lie outside their recorded extrema. The public
definitions do not establish that the summaries concern identical tablets,
units, or measurement windows. The values are therefore retained as recorded,
and the application is interpreted as historical variation rather than a
verified quality-control rule. Ties, overlapping partitions, possible batch
dependence, and exclusion of batches without final quality results further
limit a direct iid tolerance interpretation.

Tablet-core weight RSD supplies a more asymmetric placement example. MTI has
median width \PharmaRsdMtiWidth{}, compared with \PharmaRsdYmWidth{} for
Young--Mathew and \PharmaRsdWilksWidth{} for Wilks. Its median tail omissions
are \PharmaRsdMtiLowerOmitted\% below and
\PharmaRsdMtiUpperOmitted\% above, compared with
\PharmaRsdYmLowerOmitted\% and \PharmaRsdYmUpperOmitted\% for
Young--Mathew. Because larger RSD is scientifically adverse, a one-sided upper
tolerance limit would be more appropriate for a direct quality-control
decision; the two-sided result describes interval placement only.

\section{Proposed regression and dynamic endpoint computation}
\label{app:mti-extensions}

For raw endpoint functions \(\eta_{1i}\) and \(\eta_{2i}\), define
\(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\). With fixed content \(c\), tilt
\(\delta_i\), and learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the update is
\[
 \pi(\theta\mid D)
 \propto
 \exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{c,\delta}(\theta)\}\pi_0(\theta).
\]
The three quantities index different objects: interval content, placement,
and loss-update strength. This distribution is a generalized posterior for
endpoint functions, not a response likelihood.

Because the product-residual loss is invariant to exchanging the roots,
endpoint summaries sort them at each evaluation point:
\[
 L_i=\min(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\qquad
 U_i=\max(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}).
\]
For linear roots, \(\beta_m=(\beta_1+\beta_2)/2\) is label invariant and
\(\beta_s=(\beta_2-\beta_1)/2\) changes sign under a root swap, with
\[
 L(x)=x^\top\beta_m-|x^\top\beta_s|,
 \qquad
 U(x)=x^\top\beta_m+|x^\top\beta_s|.
\]

The product-residual check factor has the normal--exponential representation
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
 \exp\{-\rho_c(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\\
 &\quad=\int_0^\infty
 \Normal(e_i\mid\xi_cv_i,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)
 \Exp(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i,
\end{align*}
where
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\),
\(\xi_c=(1-2c)/\{c(1-c)\}\), and
\(\phi_c=2/\{c(1-c)\}\). Under the
\(\GIG(\nu,A,B)\) convention,
\[
 v_i\mid\cdot\sim
 \GIG\left\{\frac12,
 \frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)},
 \frac{c(1-c)e_i^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}}\right\}.
\]
The scale is computational and is not a latent response variable.

Let \(X\) denote the observed design matrix and
\(W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}\}\). Conditional on the
second root, put
\[
 A_1=\diag(y-\eta_2)X,
 \qquad z_1=y\circ(y-\eta_2)-\xi_cv.
\]
For Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((Q_{01},h_{01})\), the first root
has
\[
 Q_1=Q_{01}+A_1^\top W_vA_1,
 \qquad
 h_1=h_{01}+A_1^\top W_vz_1
       +\omega_{\mathrm R}cX^\top\delta,
\]
and \(\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim\Normal(Q_1^{-1}h_1,Q_1^{-1})\). The second-root
update exchanges labels. The fixed tilt shifts the canonical vector but not
the precision. Proper Gaussian priors give a finite normalizing constant in
finite dimension because the tilt is linear in the roots.

An ECM mode calculation replaces \(v_i^{-1}\) by
\(\{c(1-c)|e_i|\}^{-1}\), with a prespecified floor near zero, and alternates
the two Gaussian conditional systems. Each cycle is accepted only when the
exact penalized objective decreases. A conditionally Gaussian shrinkage prior
can enter through its current prior precision, but nonzero tilt with
heavy-tailed random scales requires a separate propriety argument.

Deterministic basis expansions use the same formulas after replacing \(X\) by
the fixed basis matrix; uncertainty is conditional on that construction. For
time-varying endpoints,
\[
 \eta_{jt}=f_t^\top\theta_{jt},
 \qquad
 \theta_{jt}=G_t\theta_{j,t-1}+\varepsilon_{jt},
 \qquad j=1,2.
\]
Conditional on one complete root path and the scales, the other path has a
linear Gaussian representation, which permits alternating
forward-filtering backward-sampling updates. Discount factors and component
scales belong to the state prior and must be fixed or modeled explicitly.

These extensions remain theoretical proposals: they have no matched
implementation, simulation study, or application. A future assessment must
report root-label handling, convergence or objective descent, fixed-target
loss, empirical content, width, basis or state-prior selection, and
sensitivity to initialization. Tolerance validity and response prediction
require separate, claim-matched models and validation designs.
