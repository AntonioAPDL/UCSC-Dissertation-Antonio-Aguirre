\chapter{Posterior computation and supporting evidence for Q--DESN}
\label{app:qdesn-technical}

This appendix records the nonstandard calculations and empirical
qualifications needed to interpret Chapter~\ref{ch:research-c}. Posterior
inference is conditional on the selected reservoir realization and its fixed
feature matrix. Standard Gaussian-regression calculations and complete
collections of secondary results are not reproduced.

\section{Posterior formulation}
\label{app:qdesn-posterior}

\subsection{Conventions and fixed reservoir features}

The inverse-gamma density is parameterized by
\(p(x\mid a,b)\propto x^{-a-1}\exp(-b/x)\), and
\(\GIG(\lambda,\chi,\psi)\) has kernel
\[
x^{\lambda-1}\exp\{-(\chi/x+\psi x)/2\}.
\]
For target level \(p\), define
\[
g(\gamma)=2\Phi(-|\gamma|)\exp(\gamma^2/2),\qquad
p_\gamma=\ind\{\gamma<0\}
+\frac{p-\ind\{\gamma<0\}}{g(\gamma)},
\]
and
\[
A_\gamma=\frac{1-2p_\gamma}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\qquad
B_\gamma=\frac{2}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\qquad
D_\gamma=\{\ind(\gamma>0)-p_\gamma\}^{-1}|\gamma|.
\]
At \(\gamma=0\), the exAL hierarchy reduces to AL. Away from zero,
derivatives are evaluated within the appropriate sign branch; two-sided
smoothness at zero is not assumed.

Reservoir matrices, reduction maps, leak rates, spectral radii, washout, and
the selected input specification are fixed before regression inference.
Future rows containing response lags are generated recursively and are not
treated as known in advance.

\subsection{Single-quantile target}

The exAL mixture representation is
\begin{align}
v_t\mid\sigma&\sim\Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma),&
s_t&\sim\TN(0,1),\nonumber\\
y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t,\mat X
&\sim\Normal\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+\sigma D_\gamma s_t+A_\gamma v_t,
\ \sigma B_\gamma v_t\right).
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}
\end{align}
Integrating out \((v_t,s_t)\) leaves
\(\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta\) as the conditional \(p\)-quantile. The AL
model fixes \(\gamma=0\) and omits \(s_t\).

The intercept has its own Gaussian prior. Slopes use either a Gaussian ridge
prior or the Nishimura--Suchard product form of the regularized horseshoe. For
the latter, conditional slope precision is
\(\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}+\zeta^{-2}\). The reference global scale
\(\tau_0\) is fixed from the standardized design, residual scale, sample size,
and a prespecified effective model size; it is not an estimated global scale.

\subsection{Stacked multiquantile target}

For \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\), write
\(\mu_{k,t}=\beta_{0k}+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k\). After copying
the exAL augmentation across levels and stacking the response \(K\) times,
the complete-data Gaussian factor is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{aug}}
=\phi_{KT}\!\left(
\vect y_{\mathrm{st}};
\vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}+
(\mat I_K\otimes\mat Z)\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}+\vect d,
\mat\Omega\right),
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood}
\end{equation}
where \(\mat\Omega\) is diagonal with entries
\(\sigma_kB_kv_{k,t}\). This evaluates the product of the
quantile-specific augmentation factors. Repeating the response supplies an
evaluation argument for an untempered composite update, not a normalized
\(KT\)-variate response model.

The reported MQ--DESN fits shrink the anchor
\(\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1\) and adjacent differences
\(\Delta_k=\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1}\), \(k\ge2\), with separate
regularized-horseshoe blocks. If \(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\) stacks
these quantities and \(\mat D_\Delta\) contains their conditional prior
variances, then
\begin{equation}
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\mat D_\Delta)
\propto |\mat D_\Delta|^{-1/2}
\exp\{-\tfrac12
(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}})^\top
\mat D_\Delta^{-1}(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}})\}.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_joint_qvp_prior}
\end{equation}
Ordered intercepts do not by themselves prevent crossing when slopes differ.

\section{Posterior computation}
\label{app:qdesn-computation}

Conditional on latent variables and prior scales, the single-level
coefficient block is weighted Gaussian regression. In MQ--DESN, the stacked
slope precision is
\begin{equation}
\mat K_\beta
=(\mat I_K\otimes\mat Z)^\top\mat\Omega^{-1}
(\mat I_K\otimes\mat Z)
+\mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional}
\end{equation}
Sparse factorization is used rather than a dense inverse. GIG latent-scale,
truncated-normal positive-shift, and inverse-gamma shrinkage updates have their
standard conditional forms. The bounded exAL shape is nonconjugate.

The reported multiquantile variational calculation retains a structured
\(q(\gamma)q(\sigma\mid\gamma)\) factor. For one level, let
\(e_t=y_t-\mu_t\) and denote the required current moments by
\(\bar e_t\), \(e_t^{(2)}\), \(\bar v_t\), \(v_t^-\),
\(\bar s_t\), and \(s_t^{(2)}\). Define
\begin{align}
\nu&=-a_\sigma-3T/2,\nonumber\\
\chi_\gamma&=2b_\sigma+2\sum_t\bar v_t+
B_\gamma^{-1}\sum_t
\{e_t^{(2)}v_t^- -2A_\gamma\bar e_t+A_\gamma^2\bar v_t\},\nonumber\\
\psi_\gamma&=D_\gamma^2B_\gamma^{-1}
\sum_t s_t^{(2)}v_t^-,\nonumber\\
c_\gamma&=\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)-\tfrac T2\log B_\gamma+
D_\gamma B_\gamma^{-1}
\sum_t\bar s_t(\bar e_tv_t^- -A_\gamma).
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_terms}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
q^*(\sigma,\gamma)\propto
\exp(c_\gamma)\sigma^{\nu-1}
\exp\{-\tfrac12(\chi_\gamma/\sigma+\psi_\gamma\sigma)\},
\qquad L<\gamma<U,
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_kernel}
\end{equation}
so \(q(\sigma\mid\gamma)=\GIG(\nu,\chi_\gamma,\psi_\gamma)\). If
\(Z_\sigma(\gamma)\) denotes the corresponding GIG integral, the normalized
shape factor is
\begin{equation}
q(\gamma)=
\frac{\exp(c_\gamma)Z_\sigma(\gamma)}
{(\int_L^0+\int_0^U)\exp(c_u)Z_\sigma(u)\,du}.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_structured_shape_factor}
\end{equation}
Quadrature split at zero supplies all mixed moments. Replacing them by products
of separate scale and shape expectations defines a different approximation.
For adjacent differences, the second moments retain cross-level covariance:
\[
\E\{(\mat H\vect\beta)\circ(\mat H\vect\beta)\}
=(\mat H\vect m)\circ(\mat H\vect m)
+\diag(\mat H\mat\Sigma\mat H^\top).
\]

\begin{algorithmblock}{Nonstandard Q--DESN computation}
Update the GIG latent scales and, for exAL, the positive shifts; update the
single-level or sparse stacked Gaussian coefficient block; update shrinkage
scales; update the transformed scale--shape block by MCMC or the structured
factor above; then recompute complete convergence diagnostics. For MQ--DESN,
intercepts use Gaussian conditionals restricted to their ordered support.
Convergence of an approximate objective or reported score does not establish
mixing of every original parameter coordinate.
\end{algorithmblock}

The exact Gaussian mean-regression baseline is used only to obtain stable
starting values. If \(\vect m_N\) is its posterior coefficient mean and
\(r_t^N=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_N\), the level-\(p\) readout starts at
\[
\beta_0^{\mathrm{init}}(p)=m_{N,0}+\operatorname{quantile}_p(\vect r^N),
\qquad
\beta_j^{\mathrm{init}}(p)=m_{N,j},\quad j\ge1.
\]
Its covariance may initialize a variational factor but is not an uncertainty
statement for the quantile posterior.

\section{Simulation design and sensitivity evidence}
\label{app:qdesn-simulation-support}

For fitted levels \(p_1<\cdots<p_K\), the multiquantile study orders each
reported grid by equal-weight isotonic projection. The finite-grid score is
\[
\aCRPS(y;\vect q^*)=2\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k
\rho_{p_k}(y-q_k^*),
\qquad
\omega_k=\tfrac12(p_{k+1}-p_{k-1}),
\]
with adjacent half-widths at the endpoints. On the seven-level grid the
weights sum to 0.90; the resulting values are comparable within this study,
not with a full-distribution CRPS over another probability range.

The single-quantile study uses 500 fitting observations and a primary
300-target evaluation over ten origins. Its 500-target extension contains
17 origins, with 20 scored pairs at the last origin. The longer record was
examined during development, so it is a sensitivity analysis rather than an
untouched confirmation sample. The multiquantile study uses eight mechanisms,
seven fitted levels, 500 fitting observations, and 990 forecast pairs per
mechanism. Within a mechanism, all four regressions use the same selected
reservoir realization.

\section{PriceFM comparison}
\label{app:qdesn-pricefm-support}

For each region, three successive periods inside the original training span
select reservoir, prior, and predictor settings. Outer Fold~1 validation then
selects one of Q--DESN or MQ--DESN under AL or exAL. That region-specific
choice remains fixed across all three test folds, while coefficients are
refitted on each expanding training set. Because the dates had been examined
previously, this rule does not make them unseen periods.

\section{GloFAS reference--discrepancy correction}
\label{app:qdesn-glofas-support}

Separate reservoirs represent the USGS reference process and the
GloFAS-minus-reference discrepancy. For level \(p_k\),
\begin{equation}
q_{k,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\vect\beta_k,
\qquad
d_{k,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}\top}\vect\delta_k,
\qquad
q_{k,t}^{\mathrm{glo}}=q_{k,t}+d_{k,t}.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_glofas_quantile_decomposition}
\end{equation}
This is a quantile-indexed location adjustment, not a general identity for
quantiles of differences. Historical development and confirmation periods
select the two reservoirs; the December 2022--January 2023 forecast period is
excluded from reservoir, prior, stopping, and family selection.

Within the forecast window, every future reference feature depends only on
earlier elements of the latent 28-day reference path. The product of the
future-reference factors therefore defines a normalized sequential response
distribution. Each of the \(28\times51=1{,}428\) issued-member factors has
unit weight and is conditionally independent given parameters, the path, and
preceding histories. This is a working conditional-independence assumption;
no residual member-dependence term is added.

Stack \(\vect b_k=(\vect\beta_k^\top,\vect\delta_k^\top)^\top\). The
coefficient update sums reference and GloFAS canonical terms and retains
reference--discrepancy covariance. Linearizing the random design around the
current path mean gives
\begin{equation}
q_{F,k}\approx\Normal(\vect m_k,\mat V_k),
\qquad
\mat V_k^{-1}=-\nabla^2\widetilde\ell_{F,k}+\epsilon\mat I_{28}.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_glofas_future_path_factor}
\end{equation}
For current design value \(\vect a_i^c\), Jacobian \(\mat J_i\), and path
coordinate \(\vect e_h\), the propagated moments include
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\E(\vect h_i^c\vect h_i^{c\top})
&\approx\vect a_i^c\vect a_i^{c\top}+\mat J_i\mat V\mat J_i^\top,\\
\E(\vect h_i^c y_{T+h})
&\approx\vect a_i^c m_h+\mat J_i\mat V\vect e_h.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:supp_glofas_future_path_delta}
\end{equation}
The second line retains response--design covariance; coefficient covariance
also enters the path update. The ridge \(\epsilon\mat I_{28}\) stabilizes the
local calculation but does not define a replacement response model.

The reported forecast is the marginal response quantile
\(m_{k,h}+\Phi^{-1}(p_k)\sqrt{V_{k,hh}}\), without an additional response-noise
term. Separate level-specific path factors can cross, and no projection is
applied in this application. The MQ--DESN calculation stops only after all
inner fits finish, every monitored RHS block changes by at most \(10^{-3}\),
the outer parameter change is below \(10^{-3}\), and three consecutive
complete passes satisfy these conditions. These checks assess numerical
stability, not approximation accuracy.
