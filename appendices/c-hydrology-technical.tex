\subsection{Seasonal rotations and stacked states}
\label{env:app:stackedstates}

For daily time steps, the seasonal specification uses base period
$P=363.5854$ days and frequency multipliers $h_1=1$, $h_2=2$, and
$h_3=1/6.8068493$. With $\omega_a=2\pi h_a/P$ and
\[
\mathbf R(\omega)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\omega&\sin\omega\\
-\sin\omega&\cos\omega
\end{bmatrix},
\]
the observation loading and transition are
\[
\mathbf F_t=(1,1,0,1,0,1,0)',\qquad
\mathbf G_t=\operatorname{BlockDiag}
\{1,\mathbf R(\omega_1),\mathbf R(\omega_2),\mathbf R(\omega_3)\}.
\]
The corresponding periods are approximately 363.59, 181.79, and 2474.87
days. The third is an exploratory slow rotation rather than an identified
physical cycle.

Let $\boldsymbol\omega_t'=[\zeta_t,\boldsymbol\psi_t']$ and stack
\[
\boldsymbol\eta_t'=
[\boldsymbol\theta_t',(\boldsymbol\delta_t^1)',\ldots,
(\boldsymbol\delta_t^J)',\boldsymbol\omega_t'],
\qquad q=(J+1)p+m+1.
\]
The transfer transition is
\[
\mathbf G_t^{\mathrm{trans}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda&\mathbf x_t'\\
\mathbf 0_m&\mathbf I_m
\end{bmatrix},
\]
and the full transition is block diagonal in the shared state, $J$
discrepancy states, and transfer block. If $\mathbf h_{t,0}$ selects the
shared and transfer blocks and $\mathbf h_{t,j}$ additionally selects source
$j$'s discrepancy block, then the historical observation equations are
\[
y_t^s\mid\boldsymbol\eta_t,\sigma^s,\gamma^s
\sim\operatorname{exAL}_{p_0}
(\mathbf h_{t,s}'\boldsymbol\eta_t,\sigma^s,\gamma^s),
\qquad s\in\mathcal J_0.
\]
During forecasting, the analogous selector $\mathbf e_{T+k,j}$ retains the
shared state, active source discrepancy, and transfer block. When a source
ceases to be available, states, selectors, and covariance matrices are
restricted to the retained coordinates before propagation. This restriction
accounts for unequal source horizons without treating a missing source as an
observed zero.

\subsection{Forecast-window covariance prior}
\label{env:app:forecastcovariance}

For $t>T$, let $p_t$ be the active state dimension and let $\mathbf W_T$ be
the origin covariance restricted to those coordinates. The inverse-Wishart
prior uses
\[
\mathbf S_t=(\nu_t-p_t-1)c\mathbf W_T,
\qquad \nu_t=p_t+1+\epsilon,
\]
so its contribution to the posterior mean is
\[
\mathbb E(\mathbf W_t\mid\mathcal D_t)=
\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}c\mathbf W_T+
\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}
\mathbb E(\mathbf e_t\mathbf e_t'\mid\mathcal D_t),
\]
where
$\mathbf e_t=\boldsymbol\eta_t-\mathbf M_t\boldsymbol\eta_{t-1}$.
The first term anchors the short forecast window to the fitted origin
covariance; the second allows adaptation to forecast-window state
innovations. In the reported reference fit, $c=\epsilon=1$.

\section{Predictive synthesis and score conventions}
\label{env:app:numericalprediction}
\label{app:numericalprediction}

The initial synthesized quantile curve is evaluated at 1,001 internal
probability-grid points. Sorting its values provides monotone rearrangement,
and inverse-CDF sampling produces 2,000 draws per time. Linear interpolation
is used between grid values and constant interpolation beyond the endpoints.
Beyond the fitted probabilities 0.05 and 0.95, the construction uses the
corresponding outer fitted predictive distributions. Thus the limits of a
central 95\% predictive interval are not separately fitted 0.025 and 0.975
conditional quantiles.

For ordered predictive values $x_{(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{(n)}$, individual-fit
quantiles used in isotonic projection use the interpolation rule commonly
called type 7, while stochastic-model quantiles used for calibration and
displayed interval limits use type 8 \citep{env-hyndman1996sample}. Raw
ensembles and deterministic quantile grids use their empirical generalized
inverse. These finite-representation conventions do not change the fitted
models.

For an equally weighted empirical distribution
$G_m=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m\delta_{x_i}$, write
\[
A=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m|x_i-y|,
\qquad D=\sum_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|.
\]
The exact empirical-distribution score and the separate independent-draw
adjustment are
\[
\operatorname{CRPS}(G_m,y)=A-\frac{D}{m^2},
\qquad
\operatorname{CRPS}_{\mathrm{adj}}=A-\frac{D}{m(m-1)}.
\]
The adjustment estimates the parent-distribution score only under independent,
identically distributed sampling \citep{env-ferro2014fair}; that condition is
additional for a physical ensemble. It is not applied to deterministic grids
or point masses. The quantile-grid and exact empirical scores are therefore
reported as distinct finite-representation calculations
\citep{env-zamo2018crps}. Pairing symmetric rank levels yields the weighted
interval-score representation \citep{env-bracher2021evaluating}, but does not
turn the seven fitted quantiles alone into a complete predictive distribution.

For EMOS, each lead-specific fit minimizes analytic truncated-normal CRPS
using four deterministic initial values and bounded L-BFGS-B optimization.
The converged solution with the smallest objective is retained subject to
$b,d\ge0$ and $c>0$. The eligible training cases, fitted values, and
convergence records are retained in the analysis repository.

\subsection{Conditional Gaussian augmentation}
\label{env:app:augmentation}

For fixed $p_0$, define
\[
g(\gamma)=2\Phi(-|\gamma|)\exp(\gamma^2/2),
\qquad
p(\gamma;p_0)=I(\gamma<0)+
\frac{p_0-I(\gamma<0)}{g(\gamma)}.
\]
The lower and upper skewness bounds are the negative root of
$g(\gamma)=1-p_0$ and the positive root of $g(\gamma)=p_0$. These bounds
ensure $0<p(\gamma;p_0)<1$. Let
\[
A=\frac{1-2p}{p(1-p)},\qquad
B=\frac{2}{p(1-p)},\qquad
C=\{I(\gamma>0)-p\}^{-1},
\]
where the dependence on $(\gamma,p_0)$ is suppressed. If
$e\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, $u\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$, and
$s\sim\mathcal N^+(0,1)$ are independent, then
\[
Y=\mu+C\sigma|\gamma|s+A\sigma u+
\sqrt{B\sigma^2u}\,e.
\]
With $v=\sigma u$, the conditional Gaussian variance is $B\sigma v$.
Consequently, state updates can use Gaussian filtering and smoothing
conditional on the observation-specific augmentation variables.

\subsection{Joint scale--skewness update}
\label{env:app:vblad}

Let $\boldsymbol\alpha$ contain an unconstrained log-scale and a transformed
skewness for one channel, and let $\boldsymbol\beta$ contain the remaining
unknowns. Under a mean-field factorization, define
\[
\ell(\boldsymbol\alpha)=
\mathbb E_{r(\boldsymbol\beta)}
\{\log p(\mathcal D,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)\}.
\]
The Laplace factor is
\[
r(\boldsymbol\alpha)\approx
\mathcal N(\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha},\boldsymbol\Sigma),
\quad
\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha}=\arg\max\ell(\boldsymbol\alpha),
\quad
\boldsymbol\Sigma=-\{\nabla^2\ell(\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha})\}^{-1}.
\]
For a nonlinear function $h$, the second-order Delta approximation supplies
\[
\mathbb E_r\{h(\boldsymbol\alpha)\}\approx
h(\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha})+
\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}
\{\nabla^2h(\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha})\boldsymbol\Sigma\}.
\]
These moments enter the Gaussian state and augmentation updates. Scale and
skewness remain dependent within each channel's Laplace factor, while factors
are separated across channels and from the state trajectory.

One outer iteration consists of four operations: (i) filter and smooth the
stacked Gaussian state using expected observation precisions and offsets;
(ii) update the positive generalized inverse-Gaussian augmentation variables;
(iii) update the positive truncated-normal augmentation variables; and (iv)
optimize each channel's scale--skewness block and recompute its Delta moments.
The evidence lower bound, state moments, scale moments, and skewness moments
are monitored after required minimum iteration counts. Forecast-member
observations use the same observation-level updates with their active source
selector. This description fixes the posterior approximation used in the
application without reproducing implementation-level update tables.
