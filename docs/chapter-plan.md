# Provisional chapter plan

Status: architecture only; no research repository contents audited.
The common question is provisional: how dynamic quantile and interval methods
can support interpretable uncertainty and forecasting under computational
constraints. At least three research slots are reserved; independent
contribution and program acceptance remain unverified.

| Chapter | Purpose / contribution to establish | Candidate source | Prerequisites and shared background |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction and common framework | Define the problem, targets and dissertation-level contribution map; not itself counted as a research unit. | Literature plus audited material from selected projects. | Common probability, dynamic modeling, computation and scoring definitions only where assumptions match. |
| 2. Research A: dynamic quantile forecasting | Establish the distinct methodological/computational contribution and supported forecasting evidence. | Q-DESN repository; authoritative manuscript unknown. | Relevant time-series and quantile framework from chapter 1; local model-specific assumptions. |
| 3. Research B: joint quantile inference | Establish a distinct joint-inference contribution and its relation to existing dynamic quantile work. | Joint quantile / exDQLM / exAL sources; exact allocation unknown. | Shared notation plus an explicit distinction from Barata 2021 and other antecedents. |
| 4. Research C: interval-targeted methods | Establish the actual interval target, updating rule and separate contribution. | RQR-GIBBS and/or interval-targeting material; access to itpu unresolved. | Target/coverage definitions; chapter-specific assumptions and computational framework. |
| 5. Synthesis | Compare contributions, limitations and future directions around the common question. | Only supported results from selected chapters. | No new unsupported scientific claim; not a substitute research unit. |
| Appendices | Preserve detailed derivations and supporting computational/robustness material. | Selected audited supplements and source notes. | Every appendix linked from the relevant main chapter; final bibliography follows. |

| Chapter | Necessary new exposition | Overlap risk | Evidence status / decision gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unifying narrative, contribution map, common notation and literature synthesis. | Collapsing different targets or assumptions into one framework. | Editorial planning hypothesis; author/advisor decision after inventory. |
| 2 | Dissertation context, complete assumptions and verified interpretation of experiments. | Repeated forecast data/benchmarks with chapters 3-4. | Metadata-only repository verification; no novelty or numerical claims approved. |
| 3 | Prior-work comparison, candidate/coauthor contribution statement, links to the common framework. | Particularly high: inherited exDQLM, exAL, software or joint-model material could be mistaken for new work. | Compare exact manuscript to antecedents; no independent chapter confirmed. |
| 4 | Clarify interval meaning, inferential justification, calibration claims and computation. | Multiple variants from one project may share a single central contribution. | RQR identity verified publicly; itpu identity/access unresolved; content unaudited. |
| 5 | Cross-chapter synthesis that preserves limitations and negative findings. | Repeating abstracts without integration; overstating collective guarantees. | Draft after chapter evidence and author decisions. |
| Appendices | Locators, assumptions and reproducibility context for supporting content. | Hiding essential justification outside the main argument. | Allocate only after main claims are known; remove format demonstration. |

A failed chapter gate is a real planning gap, not permission to invent another
contribution. One paper may supply multiple distinct units only if the program
and advisors agree; overlapping variants are not automatically counted twice.
