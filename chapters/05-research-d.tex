\chapter{Mean-tilted intervals and calibrated tolerance inference}
\label{ch:research-d}

\section{Introduction}
\label{rqr2026:sec:introduction}

A prescribed probability content defines a class of intervals. Equal-tailed,
shortest contiguous, and other placements may contain the same population
fraction while having different endpoints and lengths. A tolerance interval
adds a sampling requirement: with
confidence \(1-\alpha\), the random interval must contain at least a fraction
\(c\) of the population
\citep{rqr-Wilks1941ToleranceLimits,rqr-Wilks1942StatisticalPrediction,rqr-Robbins1944DistributionFreeTolerance}.
Content, placement, and tolerance confidence are distinct. Estimating the
\((1-c)/2\) and \((1+c)/2\) quantiles yields the central equal-tailed interval
\citep{qdesn-KoenkerBassett1978}; shortest and mean-preserving intervals use
different placement criteria
\citep{rqr-BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,rqr-FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-c} uses check loss to define individual conditional
quantiles. The residual-product criterion used here applies check loss to a
product residual, so the loss identifies a pair of interval endpoints. The
resulting target is a fixed-content interval whose endpoints are estimated
jointly, rather than two prespecified response quantiles.

The starting point is the residual-product criterion of
\citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. Its loss depends on
\((y-\eta_1)(y-\eta_2)\), whose sign indicates whether \(y\) falls between two
roots. First, we identify its unrestricted regular population target. Among
contiguous intervals with content \(c\), it
selects the interval whose retained mean equals the population mean. This
interval is called the mean-preserving interval (MPI); the residual-product
criterion itself is inherited from Pouplin et al.

Second, we define a placement family by adding a fixed retained-mean offset
\(\delta\). The resulting mean-tilted interval (MTI) retains
content \(c\) and has retained mean \(\mu+\delta\). MPI is the zero-tilt member,
whereas equal-tailed and shortest contiguous intervals correspond to
distribution-specific tilts. Thus \(\delta\) identifies the estimand; it is not
an ordinary model parameter to be learned without also specifying how the target
is chosen.

Third, we use this geometry to construct a tolerance procedure.
For each candidate fitted content \(q>c\) and tilt \(\delta\), a deterministic
profile calculation estimates the endpoints under a loss-based generalized
posterior criterion. A separate Dirichlet-process response-distribution posterior
assesses whether the fitted interval is likely to attain content \(c\), and the
shortest eligible candidate is selected. Simulation samples used for calibration
are separate from those used for evaluation. The independent study compares MTI
with Young--Mathew and Wilks intervals across symmetric, heavy-tailed, and
strongly skewed distributions. A pharmaceutical batch analysis then illustrates
how the methods allocate their two endpoints in a finite historical dataset.

The four claims use different forms of evidence. The population MTI
characterization is mathematical. The direct content probability is exact under
the stated Dirichlet-process model for a fixed interval. The tolerance findings
are repeated-sampling results for the prespecified rule over the eight studied
distributions. Simulation studies, numerical validation, and applications of
the regression and dynamic endpoint extensions remain future work.
Appendix~\ref{app:mti-technical} contains the supporting proofs, calibration
details, and conditional computations.

\section{Fixed-content interval targets}
\label{rqr2026:sec:interval-functionals}

Let \(Y\) have a nondegenerate continuous distribution with finite mean
\(\mu\) and strictly increasing quantile function \(Q\) on \((0,1)\). Apart
from probability-zero subsets outside the support, every contiguous interval
of content \(c\) can be represented by the probability window
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Define its retained mean and geometric width by
\[
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
Probability content fixes the length of the window on the probability scale,
but not its lower-tail index \(u\). We use ET for equal-tailed, MPI for
mean-preserving, and SH for shortest-contiguous intervals.

\section{The mean-preserving interval target}
\label{rqr2026:sec:mpi-loss}

Consider a desired interval content \(c\in(0,1)\) and ordered endpoints
\(a<b\). The residual-product criterion of
\citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR} uses the pointwise loss
\begin{equation}
\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y)=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)(y-a)(b-y), & a<y<b,\\
c(y-a)(y-b), & y\le a\ \text{or}\ y\ge b.
\end{cases}
\label{rqr2026:eq:mpi-loss-piecewise}
\end{equation}
Both branches are nonnegative and meet continuously at zero when \(y=a\) or
\(y=b\). An observation inside the interval is weighted by \(1-c\), whereas a
miss is weighted by \(c\). The two factors also encode distance from both
endpoints, so the criterion weights membership and endpoint displacement
together. Increasing \(c\) raises the relative cost of a miss; at a regular
population optimum, this weighting produces the content equation established
below. For data \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\), an unpenalized empirical MPI fit minimizes
the average loss
\(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y_i)\); using the sum gives the
same minimizer. Penalized and generalized-posterior calculations are introduced
in Section~\ref{rqr2026:sec:static-theory-statements}.

The familiar check-loss notation gives an equivalent compact representation.
For a scalar argument \(r\), let
\[
\rho_c(r)=r\{c-\ind(r<0)\}
=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)|r|, & r<0,\\
cr, & r\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
For the product residual \(e(a,b;y)=(y-a)(y-b)\), the inequality
\(e(a,b;y)<0\) holds exactly when \(a<y<b\). Hence
\begin{equation}
\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y)=\rho_c\{e(a,b;y)\}.
\label{rqr2026:eq:mpi-product-check}
\end{equation}
The loss is symmetric in its two roots; the ordering \(a<b\) is imposed only
to interpret them as lower and upper endpoints.
The check loss therefore acts on the scalar product residual, not on
\(y-a\) or \(y-b\) separately. In particular, \(c\) is the desired interval
content; it is neither an assigned response-quantile level for either endpoint
nor the generalized-Bayes learning rate.

The midpoint--half-width parameterization makes the interval geometry
explicit. Write \(a=m-h\) and \(b=m+h\), where \(h>0\). Then
\[
e(a,b;Y)=(Y-m)^2-h^2,
\]
so \(e<0\) means that \(Y\) is within distance \(h\) of \(m\).

For the conditional formulation, let \(X\) be a covariate vector and fix a
value \(x\). Write \(F_x(y)=\Pr(Y\le y\mid X=x)\),
\(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\), and \(\mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x)\). The unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class permits \(a(x)\) and \(b(x)\) to vary freely at each \(x\).
A regression class instead restricts both endpoints through parameters shared
over covariate values and therefore need not satisfy the pointwise equations.
For fixed \(x\), define
\(R_{c,x}(a,b)=\E\{\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;Y)\mid X=x\}\).
Its two first-order conditions determine content and retained-mean placement.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving score characterization]
\label{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}
Fix \(x\) and let \(a<b\). Suppose
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional distribution has no mass at \(a\)
or \(b\), differentiation may be interchanged with conditional expectation,
and \((a,b)\) is an interior stationary point over an unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class. Then the two population first-order conditions imply the
content equation
\begin{equation}
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)=c,
\label{rqr2026:eq:mpi-content-score}
\end{equation}
and the retained-mean equation
\begin{equation}
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}
=c\,\E(Y\mid X=x).
\label{rqr2026:eq:mpi-retained-mean-score}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
\end{proposition}

Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs} proves
Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization} by differentiating
the conditional risk. Its assumptions provide the integrability and
interchange conditions required for that calculation.

The population content equation is due to
\citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The retained-mean equation is a further
consequence of the same first-order system: conditional on \(X=x\), interval
membership has zero covariance with \(Y\). In omitted-tail form, the same
identity balances first-moment mass around the conditional mean:
\[
\E[\{\mu_x-Y\}\ind(Y<a)\mid X=x]
=
\E[\{Y-\mu_x\}\ind(Y>b)\mid X=x].
\]
Tail probabilities may differ; MPI balances their first moments around
\(\mu_x\). The conditional distributions of \(Y\) given \(a<Y<b\) and given its
complement consequently have mean \(\mu_x\), although the endpoint midpoint
may differ from that mean. For integrable random variables with the same mean,
write \(U\preceq_{\rm cx}V\) when
\(\E\{\varphi(U)\}\leq\E\{\varphi(V)\}\) for every convex \(\varphi\) for
which the expectations exist. Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:supp-convex-order}
in Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs} shows that the conditional distribution inside the MPI
is smaller than the full distribution in this convex order. A finite first moment is
enough for the retained-mean identity once the endpoint scores are valid. The
stronger second-moment assumption ensures finite expected MPI loss because the
loss is quadratic in the tails.

The score identities determine a quantile-window functional, which is the
object estimated by the unrestricted population loss.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{rqr2026:thm:quantile-window}
In addition to the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}, suppose that \(F_x\) is
continuous and strictly increasing on its support.
Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing. There is a unique index \(u_c(x)\)
satisfying
\[
M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x.
\]
The nondegenerate unrestricted MPI stationary interval is therefore
\[
a_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)\},\qquad
b_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}.
\]
If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(u_c(x)=(1-c)/2\), and the MPI and equal-tailed intervals coincide.
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs} proves the theorem, including the symmetry
implication, and derives the restricted score equations and nested-path
qualification. The theorem identifies the unique regular stationary window;
Theorem~\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt} below establishes global
minimization under stronger conditions. If the conditional distribution has endpoint atoms,
the corresponding directional conditions give
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x),
\]
so exact open-interval content requires endpoint continuity. Coincident roots
target the conditional mean rather than a nontrivial interval. Linear and other
restricted root classes satisfy design-weighted equations, not necessarily
Eqs.~\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-content-score}--%
\eqref{rqr2026:eq:mpi-retained-mean-score} at every \(x\). Fitted endpoints
also depend on the data, so the population identities are not finite-sample
tolerance guarantees.

\section{Mean-tilted intervals}
\label{rqr2026:sec:mti-loss}

The MTI family is the fixed-tilt generalization of MPI. It preserves the MPI
content score and replaces the zero retained-mean-balance equation by a fixed
retained-mean tilt \(\delta\), measured in response units. To move through the
content-\(c\) family without changing the coverage score, define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(m,h;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(a,b;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\]
The second form shows that the loss remains invariant to exchanging the two
roots. MPI is exactly the \(\delta=0\) member of the MTI family.

The MTI endpoint scores retain content \(c\) while shifting the retained mean.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean tilt]
\label{rqr2026:prop:mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions of
Proposition~\ref{rqr2026:prop:population-characterization}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point of the mean-tilted population risk satisfies
\[
\Pr(a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=c,\qquad
\E(Y\mid a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support, then for every
\(\delta\) strictly between \(M_c(0)-\mu\) and
\(M_c(1-c)-\mu\), its unique interior quantile-window index solves
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}

This proposition concerns stationary points. Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs}
derives its score equations and profiles the expected loss over the unique
content-\(c\) radius at each midpoint.

\begin{theorem}[Global identification of the MTI target]
\label{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt}
Suppose that the support closure is an interval (possibly with infinite
endpoints), \(F\) is absolutely continuous and strictly increasing between its
support endpoints, its density is positive and continuous on the support
interior, and \(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite
support endpoints. For every tilt satisfying
\[
M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected MTI loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered
roots. Its probability-window index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) is the unique
solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(M_c(0)<\mu<M_c(1-c)\), so MPI is the interior
special case \(\delta=0\).
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs} proves this global-minimizer result through the
profiled risk and gives the boundary analysis. Boundary tilts correspond to
one-sided probability-window limits; outside the closed admissible range there
is no finite unrestricted MTI minimizer.

A population placement tilt is the value of \(\delta\) associated with a named
fixed-content functional under a specified distribution. It is distinct from
a fixed target tilt chosen before fitting and from an empirical value selected
by a separate rule.

\begin{corollary}[Population tilts for familiar interval functionals]
\label{rqr2026:cor:recovery-tilts}
The population tilt associated with MPI is \(\delta_{\mathrm{MPI}}=0\). Define
\[
S_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u),\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
The population tilt associated with the equal-tailed interval is
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu.
\]
Each \(u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\) has associated population tilt
\(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(u)=M_c(u)-\mu\). Singular notation is used only when the
shortest-contiguous window is unique or one minimizer has been selected.
\end{corollary}

At regular interior points where the endpoint quantiles are differentiable
and have positive densities,
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)},\qquad
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Thus an interior width minimum along the tilt path satisfies the usual
equal-endpoint-density condition.
Figure~\ref{rqr2026:fig:mean-tilt-map} shows these population placements for
symmetric and skewed distributions.

These tilts are population quantities. A single tilt does not identify the same
interval functional across distributions, and conditional placement generally
requires a covariate-dependent \(\delta(x)\). The target tilt must therefore be
fixed in advance or selected by a separate rule. Positive affine response
transformations preserve the target after transforming \(\delta\); arbitrary
monotone transformations need not do so.

\section{Empirical balance and fixed-target computation}
\label{rqr2026:sec:static-theory-statements}

The population theory above concerns unrestricted fixed-content intervals. A
finite sample adds two separate questions: what the fitted interval balances on
the training data, and how a fixed-target MTI calculation is used after a
content and tilt have been chosen.

For training-sample balance, fitted endpoints depend on the observations, and
the empirical MPI/MTI loss is
nonsmooth when an observation lies on a root. The finite-sample score identity is
therefore fractional. For intercept-only MTI with fixed \(\delta\), let
\((\widehat a,\widehat b)\), \(\widehat a<\widehat b\), be an interior
subgradient-stationary empirical solution. Then there exist allocations
\(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\), equal to one for observations strictly inside the
fitted interval, zero for observations strictly outside it, and fractional only
at fitted roots, such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i y_i
=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
Thus the fitted training interval has exact fractional content and exact
fractional retained-mean balance in its empirical estimating equations.
These identities concern the unpenalized criterion. Gaussian root penalties
contribute additional terms to the estimating equations used in computation;
Section~\ref{app:mti-profile-computation} gives the corresponding penalized identities.
Population content requires a probability argument beyond this score identity.
In the iid intercept-only case, the Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality gives
a conservative high-probability population-content bound
\citep{rqr-Massart1990DKW}; Section~\ref{app:mti-proofs} gives the explicit
bound and its interpretation.

For fixed-target computation, generalized Bayes updates a prior with an
exponentiated cumulative loss rather
than a sampling likelihood \citep{rqr-BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}.
For fixed fitted content \(q\) and tilt \(\delta\), the MTI update is
\[
\pi_{q,\delta}(\theta\mid D_n)
\propto
\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{q,\delta,n}(\theta)\}\pi_0(\theta).
\]
Here \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\) is the learning rate: it fixes the scale of the
cumulative loss relative to the prior, separately from the content and tilt.
In the intercept-only calculation, \(\theta=(a,b)\), and sorting the two raw
roots gives the ordered endpoints. The update targets this fixed interval
action through its loss.

The reported calculation assigns the raw roots independent
\(\Normal(0,\lambda^{-1})\) priors with zero means. The Gaussian tails make the
finite-sample generalized posterior proper under the stated fixed-target loss;
heavier-tailed root priors require a separate argument when \(\delta\ne0\).
Writing the endpoints as \(m-h,m+h\) reduces minimization in \(h^2\) to an
empirical quantile of squared distances from \(m\). Minimizing the resulting
strongly convex midpoint profile gives the penalized-loss minimizer, which is
the mode of this generalized posterior. The reported validation
sets \(\omega_{\mathrm R}=1\), \(\lambda=10^{-4}\), and the relative
objective-gap tolerance to \(10^{-8}\); these are study specifications, not
universal defaults. Section~\ref{app:mti-profile-computation} gives the profile
identity and check.

A direct content-probability check uses the separate response-distribution
model:
\[
F\sim\operatorname{DP}(a,H),\qquad Y_i\mid F\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}F,
\]
where \(a\) is the concentration and \(H\) is the centering distribution
\citep{rqr-Ferguson1973DP}. The reported study fixes \(a=1\) and
\(H=\Normal(0,4^2)\) on the fitting scale. If
\(N_n(I)=\sum_i\ind(Y_i\in I)\), then, conditionally on the data, a fixed
interval \(I\) has the exact posterior content distribution
\[
F(I)\mid D_n\sim\operatorname{Beta}\{aH(I)+N_n(I),
a[1-H(I)]+n-N_n(I)\}.
\]
Evaluating this distribution at each fitted candidate supplies an eligibility
probability. The complete selection rule, including the comparison among
candidates, is calibrated and evaluated separately under repeated sampling.

\section{Calibrated MTI tolerance intervals}
\label{rqr2026:sec:mti-tolerance}

Let \(F\) be the true continuous response distribution, let \(D_n\) be an iid
sample from \(F\), and let the procedure return the random interval
\(\widehat T_n=[L_n,U_n]\). Its population content is
\(C_F(\widehat T_n)=F(U_n)-F(L_n)\). A content-\(c\), confidence-\(1-\alpha\)
tolerance requirement is
\[
\Pr_F^n\{C_F(\widehat T_n)\ge c\}\ge 1-\alpha.
\]
The roles of the remaining quantities are distinct. The tolerance requirement
is \((c,1-\alpha)\). Each candidate target is indexed by fitted content
\(q\in(c,1)\), which need not be a sample fraction, and placement tilt
\(\delta\). The fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) scales the loss
update, whereas the calibrated cutoff \(\gamma\) determines eligibility from
the DP content probability. Only the complete selection rule is assessed
against the tolerance requirement.

The procedure has four stages. First, a prespecified rule constructs candidate
pairs \(\{(q_j,\delta_j):j=1,\ldots,J\}\). The initial content grid is centered
on a uniform-scan retained fraction, includes fixed offsets and contents near
one, and adds an interior value whenever the effective profile mass
\(nq-\lambda/\omega_{\mathrm R}\) crosses an otherwise unrepresented rank
interval. At each content, the tilt grid contains zero, the fractional
shortest-window offset, and seven equally spaced values over the empirical
admissible range. Second, the fixed-target profile calculation gives
\(\widehat I_j=[\widehat a_j,\widehat b_j]\) for each pair. Third, the direct DP
posterior gives the content probability
\[
P_j=\Pr\{F(\widehat b_j)-F(\widehat a_j)\ge c\mid D_n\}.
\]
Finally, among candidates whose probability exceeds a calibrated cutoff
\(\gamma\), the procedure reports the shortest interval:
\[
\widehat T_n=\widehat I_{\widehat j},\qquad
\widehat j=\argmin_{j:\,P_j\ge\gamma}
\{\widehat b_j-\widehat a_j\},
\]
with the minimization restricted to finite fitted intervals. If no candidate is
eligible, the method records nonproduction rather than substituting another
interval, and validation counts that outcome as nonattainment. Equal-width
candidates are ordered by smaller fitted content, proximity to the fractional
shortest-window tilt, lower endpoint, and original candidate order.

Calibration is conducted separately for each design setting
\((n,c,1-\alpha)\). Thirty-two independent development samples
per distribution determine an initial menu of 26 cutoffs. Each cutoff is then
assessed on 24,000 fresh samples from each of eight distributions. The smallest
cutoff is retained if every run produces an interval and the simultaneous
one-sided exact binomial lower bound for content attainment is at least
\(1-\alpha\) for every distribution. Seven settings pass this initial stage.
A prespecified follow-up uses fresh development and assessment samples and 105
cutoffs for each of \((n,c)=(50,0.90)\) and \((100,0.95)\), while leaving the
seven accepted cutoffs unchanged. The two stages split the Monte Carlo error
probability \(0.05\) and use a union bound. Section~\ref{app:mti-calibration}
gives the exact allocation and selected cutoffs. Candidate construction,
hyperparameters, cutoff, and tie rules are fixed before independent validation.

Theorems~\ref{rqr2026:thm:quantile-window}--\ref{rqr2026:thm:profiled-mean-tilt} identify
the population MTI family and its admissible tilt coordinate. The exact DP
Beta distribution describes posterior content under the specified
response-distribution model. Section~\ref{rqr2026:sec:evaluation} evaluates the
complete fitted-content and tilt selection rule under repeated sampling. The
shortest-candidate property applies to the evaluated grid of numerical fits.
Selected-target endpoint uncertainty and regression-family tolerance
calibration require additional results.

\section{Validation design}
\label{rqr2026:sec:evaluation}

The validation study assesses population content attainment and interval width
under repeated sampling. The population MTI identities identify the target
family, while the simulation study evaluates the complete fitted-content and
tilt selection rule.

The tolerance study evaluates the selected MTI rule on samples independent
of its calibration, following simulation-study reporting principles
\citep{rqr-MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}. It uses iid continuous samples from
eight centered and scaled simulation distributions. Gaussian and Student \(t_3\)
samples serve as symmetric reference distributions. The asymmetric simulation
distributions are exponential,
asymmetric Laplace with \(\tau=0.10\), a two-piece Normal distribution with scale ratio
\(1{:}12\), Beta\((1,8)\), Gamma\((0.5,1)\), and log-normal with log-scale
standard deviation \(1.25\). This panel is intentionally skewed toward
asymmetric distributions to examine interval placement when equal-tail or full-range
behavior can be inefficient.
Multimodal cases are excluded because none of the reported methods is designed
to recover disconnected high-density regions.
The primary simulation design fixes tolerance confidence at \(0.95\) and uses
1000 replications per method, simulation distribution, and design setting.
MTI, Young--Mathew, and Wilks use the same samples in every replication.
These validation samples are independent of both threshold development and
calibration assessment, allowing paired comparisons of attainment and width.
The nine retained settings are \(n=50,c=0.90\),
\(n=100,c\in\{0.90,0.95\}\), and
\(n\in\{500,1000\},c\in\{0.90,0.95,0.99\}\).

The small-sample restriction is structural. For a two-sided distribution-free
interval, even the full sample range reaches content \(c\) with confidence
\(0.95\) only when
\[
  1-n(1-c)c^{n-1}-c^n \ge 0.95 .
\]
For intervals contained in the sample range, the corresponding minimum sample
sizes are \(46\), \(93\), and \(473\) for
\(c=0.90\), \(0.95\), and \(0.99\), respectively. Thus
\(n=50,c=0.95\) and \(n=100,c=0.99\) are excluded from the confirmatory
comparison because the sample-bounded order-statistic comparators cannot
support the requested statement in those settings. These thresholds apply to
the design's sample-bounded order-statistic reference class. The retained
\(c=0.99\) settings begin at \(n=500\), the
nearest rounded sample size above the \(n=473\) threshold.

MTI is the proposed method in the comparison. It uses a calibration rule indexed
by sample size and requested content. Within each setting, the rule fixes a direct
Dirichlet-process content-probability check from an independent calibration run, profiles
fixed-target MTI endpoint fits over fitted content and retained-mean tilt,
and keeps the shortest candidate satisfying the content-probability check.
The cutoff is selected by the simultaneous calibration bounds in
Section~\ref{rqr2026:sec:mti-tolerance}; the scan provides an initial fitted-content
reference. Tolerance performance is assessed on the fresh validation samples.

Young--Mathew interpolation
\citep{rqr-YoungMathew2014NonparametricTolerance} and Wilks min--max intervals
\citep{rqr-Wilks1941ToleranceLimits,rqr-Wilks1942StatisticalPrediction} provide the
classical order-statistic comparators. The primary metric is the
content-attainment rate: the Monte Carlo estimate of
\(\Pr_F^n\{C_F(\widehat T_n)\ge c\}\), where
\(C_F([L,U])=F(U)-F(L)\). For each replication, this content check uses the
known simulation distribution rather than the fraction of sampled observations
inside the produced interval. If a method produced no interval in a replication,
that replication would be counted as nonattainment. Widths are reported as
distribution-level empirical 2.5\%--97.5\% ranges, avoiding width averages
across distributions with different scales and tail behavior.
Figure~\ref{rqr2026:fig:tolerance-validation-width-ranges} also marks the population
shortest content-\(c\) interval for each simulation distribution. This
population benchmark supplies a lower reference for interval width.

\section{Regression and dynamic endpoint extensions}
\label{mti:sec:extensions}

The fixed-target MTI loss also defines endpoint functions when interval
placement varies with predictors or time. These extensions estimate a chosen
content--tilt target. Response-distribution modeling and tolerance calibration
remain separate tasks. For observations
\(D_n=\{(x_i,y_i):i=1,\ldots,n\}\), let the two raw endpoint functions be
\(\eta_1(x;\theta_1)\) and \(\eta_2(x;\theta_2)\). Given fixed content \(c\),
tilt \(\delta\), learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), and prior
\(\pi_0\), the generalized posterior is
\begin{equation}
\pi(\theta_1,\theta_2\mid D_n)
\propto
\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{c,\delta,n}(\theta_1,\theta_2)\}
\pi_0(\theta_1,\theta_2).
\label{mti:eq:generalized-posterior}
\end{equation}
The learning rate controls the scale of the loss update and is distinct from
content, tilt, and any response-distribution parameter.

For static linear endpoint regression, the raw roots are
\(\eta_j(x)=x^\top\beta_j\), \(j=1,2\). Because the loss is unchanged when the
root labels are exchanged, interpretation is based on sorted fitted endpoints
\[
L(x)=\min\{\eta_1(x),\eta_2(x)\},\qquad
U(x)=\max\{\eta_1(x),\eta_2(x)\}.
\]
Equivalently, midpoint and signed-gap coefficients
\(\beta_m=(\beta_1+\beta_2)/2\) and
\(\beta_s=(\beta_2-\beta_1)/2\) give
\(L(x)=x^\top\beta_m-|x^\top\beta_s|\) and
\(U(x)=x^\top\beta_m+|x^\top\beta_s|\).
A coefficient-wise lower/upper interpretation is justified only over a design
region where the root ordering does not change.

A normal--exponential representation of the product-residual check loss gives
generalized-inverse-Gaussian latent-scale updates and a Gaussian conditional
update for either root given the other. It supports Gibbs computation and a
deterministic ECM mode calculation under proper Gaussian priors. The
representation augments the loss factor in
Equation~\eqref{mti:eq:generalized-posterior}.
Section~\ref{app:mti-extensions} derives the latent-scale distribution, Gaussian
canonical parameters, fixed-tilt shift, and the conditions under which the
conditional update is well defined.

Deterministic basis expansions use the same equations after replacing the
design rows by fixed basis vectors. Time-varying endpoints instead use two
root-specific state trajectories,
\[
\eta_{jt}=f_t^\top\theta_{jt},\qquad
\theta_{jt}=G_t\theta_{j,t-1}+\varepsilon_{jt},\qquad j=1,2,
\]
with Gaussian state innovations. Conditional on one complete root path and the
latent scales, the other path has a linear Gaussian representation, motivating
alternating forward-filtering backward-sampling updates. The conditional
derivation is given in Section~\ref{app:mti-extensions}.

Simulation studies, numerical validation, and applications of the regression
and dynamic extensions remain future work.
Endpoint regression should be evaluated using recovery of known endpoint
functions, held-out MTI loss, empirical content, and width. A dynamic analysis
must additionally state its basis or state prior and the rule used to select
discount factors. Repeated-sampling tolerance behavior should be assessed
through an action-matched calibration study, and predictive analysis requires
a specified response-distribution model.

\section{Discussion}
\label{rqr2026:sec:discussion}

Probability content determines a class of interval placements. Under the stated
population conditions, MPI selects the member whose retained mean equals the
population mean. MTI makes retained-mean balance an explicit coordinate:
\(\delta=0\) returns MPI, while admissible nonzero tilts specify other
contiguous content-\(c\) windows. Equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous
intervals are distribution-specific members of this placement family rather
than universally equivalent targets.

The calibrated procedure uses this family to search for short tolerance
intervals. Fixed-target endpoint estimates are combined with a direct
Dirichlet-process posterior content check, and a separate calibration run fixes
the selection rule before validation. In the independent study, empirical
content attainment exceeds the nominal tolerance confidence in every reported
setting, and MTI has smaller mean width than Young--Mathew in two thirds of the
asymmetric distribution--setting cases. The independent confidence bounds
remain inconclusive in 34 of the 72 cases. These findings characterize the
prespecified procedure over the stated eight-distribution design.

The inferential target, computational update, content probability, and
tolerance-confidence statement have separate roles. MTI identifies the
fixed-content interval being estimated, and the generalized posterior describes
uncertainty for that loss-defined target. The Dirichlet-process response model
gives content probabilities for the eligibility screen. Repeated-sampling
content attainment evaluates the selected tolerance action.

The pharmaceutical analysis has a narrower, descriptive role. It shows how the
precalibrated procedures place endpoints on historical batch measurements and
how held-out content changes with the partition. Overlapping splits, possible
batch dependence, ties, unresolved measurement definitions, and shared
production campaigns restrict its interpretation for future production.
Prospective evaluation on independent future batches, with the measurement
definition and production grouping recorded in advance, is needed to assess
performance beyond this historical dataset.

The tolerance evidence covers iid continuous univariate simulations, and
boundary tilts can lead to one-sided limits or inactive roots. The regression
and dynamic endpoint models generalize the loss-defined target. Their empirical
assessment requires implementations, numerical diagnostics, simulation designs,
and target-specific calibration.
