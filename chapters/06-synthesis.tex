\chapter{Synthesis, limitations and future work}
\label{ch:synthesis}

\section{Relationships among the four contributions}
\label{sec:synthesis-target-first}

The four research chapters address related distributional questions but do not
estimate the same object. Chapter~\ref{ch:research-b} studies correction and
synthesis of river-flow forecasts from heterogeneous sources.
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-a} develops software for dynamic and static Bayesian
quantile analysis. Chapter~\ref{ch:research-c} uses fixed nonlinear recurrent
features as predictors in single-level and multiquantile regression through
the quantile deep echo state network (Q--DESN).
Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d} turns from conditional quantiles to the placement
of contiguous fixed-content intervals through the mean-tilted interval (MTI)
construction and to their use in tolerance inference.

Table~\ref{tab:synthesis-comparison} summarizes the principal statistical
distinctions. The hydrology, software, and Q--DESN chapters use asymmetric
Laplace (AL) or extended asymmetric Laplace (exAL) likelihoods to study
conditional quantiles. Some analyses then combine separately fitted quantiles
to approximate a predictive distribution. The MTI chapter instead begins with
a residual-product loss for two interval endpoints and evaluates a separate
tolerance action by repeated sampling. Similar latent-variable calculations
therefore support different interpretations in the four projects.

There are two direct methodological links. First, the hydrologic model is an
application and extension of the exDQLM methods supported by \pkg{exdqlm}; its
source-specific discrepancies and forecast-window synthesis address the
structure of the river-flow problem. Second, Q--DESN retains Bayesian quantile
regression but replaces linear state evolution in the quantile location with a
fixed nonlinear temporal design. The MTI project is related through
loss-based distributional inference, yet its two endpoints are not assigned
quantile levels in advance. Its content, placement, and tolerance confidence
must be analyzed separately.

\section{Principal findings}
\label{sec:synthesis-findings}

\subsection{Source-aware hydrologic correction and synthesis}

The hydrologic study combines a shared latent quantile process with
source-specific discrepancies for the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)
observations, retrospective model products, and issued ensemble forecasts.
Transfer covariates carry information
from the pre-origin period into the forecast window, after which fitted
quantiles are synthesized to obtain complete predictive distributions.

Under the single specification used for all five retained forecast origins,
the multivariate exAL model with an active transfer component has the lowest
28-day continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) among the Bayesian variants
at every origin. The separate eight-day comparison against National Weather
Service (NWS) products is more heterogeneous. The five-origin evidence favors
source-specific correction and synthesis for this target, but not every
comparator at every origin or horizon. Forecasts condition on one supplied
precipitation and soil-water path at each origin. The study is limited to five
origins because every 28-day window requires repeated long-record fitting,
extensive forecast-product post-processing, and substantial computation,
storage, and elapsed time. A matched full-model Markov chain Monte Carlo
comparison remains necessary to assess the variational approximation directly.

\subsection{Statistical computation for dynamic quantile models}

The exAL family and the original exDQLM model, Markov chain Monte Carlo
procedures, importance-sampling variational Bayes (ISVB), and transfer-function
extension precede the software article. Version 0.1.1 of the original package
provided dynamic model construction, MCMC, ISVB, transfer-function ISVB,
forecasting, diagnostic checks, and plotting. Our contribution is the substantial
later restructuring and extension of \pkg{exdqlm}: improved
Laplace--Delta variational Bayes (LDVB) and nonconjugate scale--skewness updates;
compiled implementations for intensive operations, with \proglang{R}
implementations retained where applicable; corrections to filtering and
smoothing; forecasting, diagnostics, scoring, and posterior-predictive
synthesis; and static AL/exAL regression under regularized-horseshoe shrinkage.

The examples examine distinct computational and inferential properties. The Lake Huron
analysis combines multi-quantile fitting and predictive synthesis; the
Sunspots example compares DQLM and exDQLM computation; and the sparse
simulation compares MCMC with LDVB under the same static model. LDVB is faster
in the reported sparse fits, whereas MCMC has lower held-out quantile error at
all three levels. Convergence, approximation quality, uncertainty, and
predictive accuracy are assessed by separate diagnostics.

\subsection{Nonlinear recurrent features for conditional quantiles}

Q--DESN uses a fixed deep echo-state network to construct nonlinear temporal
predictors and then estimates conditional quantiles by regularized Bayesian
regression. The multiquantile Q--DESN (MQ--DESN) shares information across
levels through shrinkage on adjacent coefficient differences, which promotes
similar neighboring effects. We do not impose explicit noncrossing constraints.

In the simulations, the independent regressions have the lowest mean oracle
score in six of eight mechanisms. Relative to independent fits, adjacent-level
regularization reduces the crossing rate from 5.488\% to 0.021\% under AL and
from 0.606\% to 0\% under exAL. The observed tradeoff is therefore improved
ordering without a uniform improvement in quantile recovery. The results are
conditional on the selected reservoirs and realized evaluation trajectories.
In the retrospective Global Flood Awareness System (GloFAS) application, all
six fitted models improve on the raw ensemble over the 28 issued horizons, and
the four quantile regressions improve on both Gaussian baselines. Q--DESN under
AL has the lowest grid score, 50.2\% below the raw ensemble. The GloFAS comparison
conditions on realized future weather.
In the PriceFM comparison, region-specific validation selects among Q--DESN
and MQ--DESN under AL and exAL. Over 114 region--fold cases, the selected DESN
models have mean AQL 6.687, compared with 7.039 for
reproduced PriceFM forecasts, and have a lower mean in each fold. The comparison
contrasts Bayesian regression on fixed recurrent features with joint estimation
of recurrent-network and readout weights. The regressions use supplied day-ahead load,
solar, and wind forecasts as future covariates.

\subsection{Fixed-content interval placement and tolerance inference}

The MTI project begins with the residual-product criterion and population-content
equation of \citet{rqr-PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. Its retained-mean characterization
identifies the mean-preserving interval under the stated regularity conditions.
A retained-mean tilt then indexes other contiguous intervals having the same
content. Equal-tailed and shortest contiguous intervals correspond to
distribution-specific tilts rather than to one universal value.

The generalized posterior supports Gibbs sampling for fixed-target endpoint
uncertainty and ECM for mode estimation. The tolerance procedure searches
over fitted content and tilt using ECM estimates and a direct
Dirichlet-process content probability to determine eligibility. Its cutoff is
calibrated before evaluation on independent samples. Across 72
distribution--setting cases, MTI--ECM content attainment ranges from
95.1\% to 99.9\%; its mean width is smaller than that of Young--Mathew in 39
cases. The closest cases remain statistically compatible with the nominal
0.95 confidence, so the study supplies repeated-sampling evidence for the
specified finite-iteration rule over the eight-distribution panel. Of the
72,000 selected ECM fits, 29,884 reach the iteration cap; all are retained in
the comparison. The pharmaceutical
application reports endpoint placement, width, empirical content, and
partition sensitivity on historical batches. Its overlapping partitions,
possible dependence, ties, and unresolved measurement definitions make this a
descriptive analysis. A prospective evaluation would use independent future
batches, a pre-recorded measurement definition, and a fixed grouping rule.
Simulation studies, numerical validation, and applications of the regression
and dynamic endpoint models remain future work.

\section{Statistical distinctions across projects}
\label{sec:synthesis-lessons}

\subsection{Uncertainty is defined by the inferential object}

A credible interval for a coefficient, a band for a fitted conditional
quantile, a predictive interval for a future response, and a tolerance interval
are not interchangeable. In the first three research chapters, posterior draws
may describe model parameters, latent quantile states, or future responses
after a specified predictive calculation. In Chapter~\ref{ch:research-d}, the
generalized posterior describes endpoints for a fixed loss-defined target. A
tolerance statement then concerns the repeated-sampling behavior of the
complete interval-selection rule.

A fitted interval inherits its interpretation from its target and validation
criterion. Two fitted conditional quantiles define an equal-tailed interval
when their probability levels are selected for that purpose. A tolerance
claim additionally requires a probability model, synthesis step, calibration
argument, or evaluation design for the complete interval rule.

\subsection{Computation and statistical evidence require different checks}

Posterior simulation, variational approximation, and deterministic profiling
solve different computational problems. MCMC diagnostics address simulation
from a specified posterior. Evidence lower bound (ELBO) behavior and paired comparisons with MCMC
assess aspects of a variational approximation. Predictive scores and
calibration summaries assess fitted forecasts. Independent simulations of
population content assess a tolerance action. No one diagnostic establishes
all of these properties.

The empirical comparisons also have different scopes. The hydrology study uses
five retrospective origins under one common specification. The Q--DESN studies
condition on selected fixed reservoirs and use bounded retrospective
applications. The MTI evaluation separates calibration from independent
simulation, but covers a finite set of distributions and sample-size/content
settings. These designs support correspondingly different conclusions.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:synthesis-limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textit{Working-likelihood uncertainty.} AL and exAL likelihoods provide
tractable conditional-quantile models, but posterior spread under
misspecification needs further repeated-sampling assessment. Sandwich
corrections and calibrated loss scales are possible directions.

\item \textit{Dependence across quantile levels.} Separate fits followed by
synthesis or rearrangement do not define a joint posterior over
quantile-specific parameters. MQ--DESN shares information across levels but
does not impose explicit noncrossing constraints.

\item \textit{Forecast-design breadth.} The hydrologic study contains five
origins, whereas the Q--DESN GloFAS application contains one. The PriceFM
comparison uses three folds but relies on region-specific model selection.
Broader forecast assessment requires more origins and additional prospective
evaluations.

\item \textit{Approximation and feature uncertainty.} Variational posterior
spread requires more systematic comparison with posterior simulation. The
Q--DESN posterior intervals condition on selected reservoirs and do not include
variation from reservoir construction or selection.

\item \textit{Tolerance scope.} The calibrated MTI results are empirical for
the studied distributional panel. Arbitrary distributions, discrete responses,
dependence, and conditional or dynamic tolerance statements require additional
theory and experiments.

\item \textit{External validity.} Every application is tied to a finite dataset
and a particular information set. Extension to other populations, periods, and
operating conditions must be evaluated rather than assumed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Directions for future research}
\label{sec:synthesis-future}

One direction is a coherent dynamic quantile model that combines
source-specific discrepancy correction with nonlinear recurrent features. Such
a model would need either explicit noncrossing constraints or a probabilistic
quantile-process construction and should be compared with simpler separate
fits under the same forecast origins and inputs.

A second direction is uncertainty calibration for AL and exAL analyses.
Repeated-sampling studies of fitted quantiles and posterior intervals could
identify when the likelihood-based spread is adequate and when sandwich or
loss-scale corrections are necessary. Paired MCMC and variational fits under
identical model specifications would separate approximation error from
model-selection differences.

A third direction is broader hydrologic evaluation. A dense archive of
version-consistent forecast origins would support stronger comparisons, while
a probabilistic model for precipitation and soil-water forecasts would
propagate exogenous uncertainty. Extension across gauges or basins could then
test whether source-specific discrepancy information transfers spatially.

A fourth direction concerns adaptive fixed-content intervals. The MTI tolerance
rule should be studied under additional distributions, dependence, ties, and
alternative content models. Implementing the proposed regression and dynamic
endpoint formulations would establish their numerical behavior, but tolerance
claims would still require target-specific calibration and repeated-sampling
evaluation.

\section{Concluding perspective}
\label{sec:synthesis-conclusion}

The four projects provide complementary methods for non-mean distributional
inference. The hydrologic model estimates and synthesizes source-corrected
conditional quantiles; \pkg{exdqlm} supplies the posterior computation,
diagnostics, and prediction needed to fit dynamic and static quantile models;
Q--DESN extends quantile regression to fixed nonlinear recurrent features and
quantifies the observed tradeoff between quantile ordering and recovery; and
MTI separates the placement of a fixed-content interval from the calibration
of a repeated-sampling tolerance statement. In each project, the statistical
target determines the model, the required computation, and the evidence that
can validate the resulting claim. Across the four projects, the inferential
target also determines the relevant uncertainty statement and validation:
predictive scores for forecast distributions, posterior diagnostics and
matched computations for approximate inference, and repeated-sampling content
for tolerance procedures. Open questions concern posterior approximation,
forecast-design breadth, and prospective validation.
