\section{Introduction}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:intro}

Conditional quantiles describe tail risk and provide forecasts suited to
asymmetric decision losses and prediction intervals
\citep{qdesn-KoenkerBassett1978,qdesn-Koenker2005,qdesn-Gneiting2011}. Let
\(\{Y_s:s\geq1\}\) be the response time series, with observed realizations
\(y_1,\ldots,y_o\) at forecast origin \(o\). Write \(\mathcal F_o\) for the
response and covariate history available at that origin. The level-\(p\)
conditional quantile at horizon \(h\geq1\) is
\[
\text{Forecast quantile:}\quad
Q_{o,h}(p)=\inf\{u:\Pr(Y_{o+h}\le u\mid\mathcal F_o)\ge p\},
\qquad 0<p<1.
\]
When future covariates are supplied, the forecast is also conditional on that
specified path; Section~\ref{qdesn2026:sec:forecast} distinguishes those inputs
from future responses generated recursively. Conditional quantiles can depend
nonlinearly on the observed histories, but Bayesian regression with unknown
recurrent-network weights can be costly when many probability levels or
forecast origins must be fitted.

The quantile deep echo state network (Q--DESN) is a Bayesian quantile
regression whose temporal predictors are fixed nonlinear recurrent states.
These predictors are called reservoir states: their internal weights are
drawn before regression fitting and then held fixed. Conditional on the
resulting features, the quantile location is linear in the final regression
coefficients. Nonlinear temporal dependence can therefore enter without a
posterior distribution for every recurrent weight.

This project makes three related contributions \citep{qdesn-deleon2026qdesn}.
First, Q--DESN combines fixed deep echo-state features with AL or exAL Bayesian
quantile regression, ridge or regularized-horseshoe (RHS) coefficient priors,
and model-specific MCMC and variational approximations. Second, the
multiquantile extension (MQ--DESN) estimates several quantile regressions
together using RHS priors on adjacent slope differences. This regularization
encourages similar neighboring effects but does not impose noncrossing. Third,
the methods are evaluated through linear and nonlinear simulation mechanisms,
retrospective streamflow correction, and regional electricity-price
forecasting. The studies assess quantile recovery, forecast loss, crossings,
and cost without supporting a uniform ranking.

Echo state networks generate dynamic predictors from recurrent states whose
internal weights are fixed before fitting
\citep{qdesn-Jaeger2001EchoState,qdesn-LukoseviciusJaeger2009ReservoirComputing}.
Stacking reservoirs produces a deep echo state network
\citep{qdesn-GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign}. Bayesian regression on
reservoir features has precedent in the hierarchical Bayesian DESN of
\citet{qdesn-McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}.

The asymmetric Laplace (AL) likelihood connects its location to a specified
conditional quantile and admits a normal--exponential augmentation
\citep{env-yu2001bayesian,qdesn-KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}. Its coefficient
credible intervals need not have nominal frequentist coverage under
misspecification \citep{qdesn-YangWangHe2016ALInference}. The exAL likelihood
of \citet{exd-yan2025family} adds a shape parameter while preserving the
quantile location. Ridge uses a common coefficient scale, whereas RHS permits
coefficient-specific shrinkage subject to a slab bound
\citep{qdesn-CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,qdesn-PiironenVehtari2017RHS}. The
mixture representations support MCMC, and model-specific variational
approximations reduce the cost of repeated fitting.

Quantile reservoir methods also have precedent.
\citet{qdesn-LvZhaoLiuWang2016QRESNE} estimate ESN output weights by quantile
regression and combine separate fits in a bootstrap ensemble.
\citet{qdesn-BonasWikleCastruccio2024CalibratedDeepESN} first fit DESN point
forecasts, then estimate lead-dependent residual quantiles with penalized
quantile sheets and calibrate the resulting intervals. Q--DESN instead
estimates response quantiles through Bayesian regression on fixed DESN
features. MQ--DESN estimates the quantile readouts jointly within one
composite regression. Neither construction requires a second-stage
residual-quantile regression or bootstrap ensemble.

Independent quantile curves can cross. Monotone rearrangement and isotonic
regression correct crossings after estimation
\citep{qdesn-BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,exd-chernozhukov2010,qdesn-DeLeonPradoSanso2026HydrologicProducts},
whereas constraints or monotone quantile-process models impose ordering during
estimation
\citep{qdesn-WuLiu2009StepwiseNCQR,qdesn-BondellReichWang2010NoncrossingQR,qdesn-YangTokdar2017JointQuantilePlanes}.
MQ--DESN instead adapts the continuous-shrinkage construction of
\citet{qdesn-KohnsSzendrei2025QVP}. RHS priors on adjacent slope differences
reduce predictor-dependent variation in neighboring gaps. Together with
ordered intercepts, they favor nearly parallel curves without ensuring that
every fitted gap is nonnegative.

Chapters~\ref{ch:research-b} and~\ref{ch:research-a} use evolving linear states
for dynamic quantile inference and hydrologic product synthesis. Q--DESN uses
fixed nonlinear recurrent predictors instead, while MQ--DESN also couples
coefficients across probability levels. The evidence later in this chapter
asks whether these predictors recover conditional quantile paths under linear
and nonlinear mechanisms, how independent and multiquantile regressions score
on a common grid, and how they perform in streamflow correction and
electricity-price forecasting. Sections~\ref{qdesn2026:sec:model}--%
\ref{qdesn2026:sec:forecast} define the model, computation, and forecast
evaluation. Sections~\ref{qdesn2026:sec:simulation}--%
\ref{qdesn2026:sec:pricefm-application} present the evidence. Posterior
derivations and supporting results are in Sections~\ref{app:qdesn-posterior}--%
\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support} of Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-technical}.

\section{Fixed-feature Bayesian quantile models}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:model}

\subsection{Single-level quantile regression}

Let \(Y_t\), \(t=1,\ldots,T\), be the response, with observed value
\(y_t\), and let
\(\vect x_t=(1,\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top)^\top\in\R^{r+1}\) be a specified
vector containing an intercept and \(r\) temporal predictors. Write
\(\vect\beta=(\beta_0,\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}}^\top)^\top\in\R^{r+1}\).
At probability level \(p_0\in(0,1)\), the linear quantile regression is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Quantile location:}\quad &\mu_t=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta,\\
\text{Likelihood:}\quad
&Y_t\mid\vect x_t,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma
  \sim\exAL_{p_0}(\mu_t,\sigma,\gamma),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:qdesn-marginal-main}
\end{equation}
Under this likelihood, \(\mu_t\) is the conditional \(p_0\)-quantile,
\(\sigma>0\) controls scale, and \(\gamma\in(L,U)\) controls shape. The
shape interval \((L,U)\) is determined by \(p_0\). Setting \(\gamma=0\)
recovers the AL regression. The scale has a proper inverse-gamma prior and
exAL uses a proper shape prior on \((L,U)\); the numerical settings are stated
with each analysis. Section~\ref{qdesn2026:subsec:desn-features} constructs
\(\vect x_t\) from response and covariate histories. Conditional on that
design, the location in Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:qdesn-marginal-main} is linear
in \(\vect\beta\).

\subsection{Deep echo state network features}
\label{qdesn2026:subsec:desn-features}

Let \(\vect z_t\in\R^q\) contain the exogenous covariates. With maximum
response lag \(m\geq1\) and maximum covariate lag \(m_z\geq0\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Lagged input:}\quad
\vect u_t&=(y_{t-1},\ldots,y_{t-m},
\vect z_t^\top,\ldots,\vect z_{t-m_z}^\top)^\top,\\
\text{Input dimension:}\quad d_u&=m+q(m_z+1).
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:desn-input-main}
\end{equation}
The current covariate \(\vect z_t\) may enter only when it is observed or
otherwise supplied for the analysis; no contemporaneous response enters
\(\vect u_t\). In a network with \(D\) layers, the reservoir state
\(\vect h_{t,d}\in\R^{n_d}\) is a recurrent nonlinear transformation of
these inputs. For a square matrix \(\mat W\), let \(\rho(\mat W)\) be its
largest absolute eigenvalue. A sparse drawn recurrent matrix \(\mat W_d\)
with nonzero radius is scaled to the selected radius \(\rho_d\) by
\(\bar{\mat W}_d=\rho_d\mat W_d/\rho(\mat W_d)\).
The matrix \(\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\) contains fixed input weights and
\(\alpha_d\in(0,1]\) is the leak rate. Between layers,
\(\mat Q_{d-1}\in\R^{\tilde n_{d-1}\times n_{d-1}}\) gives
\(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}=\mat Q_{d-1}\vect h_{t,d-1}\).
The activation \(f\) acts componentwise. For a reduced state, the readout map
\(k_d:\R^{\tilde n_d}\to\R^{s_d}\) defines the retained feature block; the
reported analyses use the identity map, so \(s_d=\tilde n_d\).
With a fixed first-layer input bias \(\vect b_1^{\mathrm{in}}\), the
recursion and general feature vector are
\begin{equation}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
\text{First layer:}\quad &\vect h_{t,1}
&&=(1-\alpha_1)\vect h_{t-1,1}\\
&&&\quad+\alpha_1 f(\bar{\mat W}_1\vect h_{t-1,1}
+\mat W_1^{\mathrm{in}}\vect u_t+\vect b_1^{\mathrm{in}}),\\
\text{Higher layers:}\quad &\vect h_{t,d}
&&=(1-\alpha_d)\vect h_{t-1,d}\\
&&&\quad+\alpha_d f(\bar{\mat W}_d\vect h_{t-1,d}
+\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}),\quad d=2,\ldots,D,\\
\text{Features:}\quad &\tilde{\vect x}_t
&&=\big[\vect h_{t,D}^\top,k_1(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,1})^\top,\ldots,
k_{D-1}(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,D-1})^\top\big]^\top.
\end{alignedat}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:desn-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\mat W}_d\in\R^{n_d\times n_d}\),
\(\mat W_1^{\mathrm{in}}\in\R^{n_1\times d_u}\),
\(\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\in\R^{n_d\times\tilde n_{d-1}}\) for \(d>1\),
and \(\vect b_1^{\mathrm{in}}\in\R^{n_1}\), so every matrix product in
Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:desn-hierarchy} has dimension \(n_d\).
A small leak rate retains more of the previous state. The displayed feature
vector has dimension \(r=n_D+\sum_{d<D}s_d\). An analysis may append direct inputs,
in which case \(r\) increases by the number appended; the reported streamflow
readouts contain reservoir features and an intercept but no direct input
regressors. Appending the intercept gives \(\vect x_t\).

Reservoir and input weights are random draws made before regression fitting
and then treated as fixed. Depth, widths, sparsity, input scaling, spectral
radii, leak rates, reduction maps, transformations, and washout are tuning or
design quantities selected using the stated fitting/development information.
The regression coefficients and likelihood parameters are inferred
conditional on those choices. The washout discards early states to reduce
dependence on their initial values.

The predictors used in each simulation or application are selected reservoir
layers and, where stated, direct inputs.
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support} records how specification
selection limits the independence of the simulation evidence. For observed fitting inputs, the resulting design matrix
\(\mat X=(\vect x_1,\ldots,\vect x_T)^\top\) is fixed. Future rows follow
the forecasting rule in Section~\ref{qdesn2026:sec:forecast}.

\subsection{Coefficient regularization}

The intercept has a separately specified Gaussian prior on the response scale
and is excluded from slope shrinkage. Intercept calibration and any ordering
restrictions for multiquantile fits are stated with each study. For the \(r\)
non-intercept
coefficients, ridge regularization uses
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}}\sim
\Normal(\zeros{r},\kappa_\beta^2\Id{r})\), with common prior standard
deviation \(\kappa_\beta\). The adaptive alternative uses the
Nishimura--Suchard product form of the regularized horseshoe
\citep{qdesn-MakalicSchmidt2016SimpleSampler,exd-nishimura2023shrunken}. For the
\(j\)th slope \(\beta_j\), conditional on global, local, and slab scales
\((\tau,\lambda_j,\zeta)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Slope prior:}\quad
&\beta_j\mid\tau,\lambda_j,\zeta\sim\Normal(0,V_j),\\
\text{Effective variance:}\quad
&V_j=\left(\zeta^{-2}+\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}\right)^{-1},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,r.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:rhs-var}
\end{equation}
The local and global scale factors use the half-Cauchy auxiliary hierarchy,
with reference scales one and \(\tau_0\), respectively; \(\zeta^2\) bounds the
coefficient variances in Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:rhs-var}. The resulting
slab-bounded precision and its role in the fitted hierarchy are summarized in
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-posterior}.

The fixed hyperprior scale \(\tau_0\) controls prior shrinkage through the
random global scale \(\tau\). For standardized predictors, a Gaussian
regression calculation provides a reference for \(\tau_0\) from a residual standard
deviation, fitting-sample size, and a prior reference number of coefficients
expected to escape strong shrinkage \citep{qdesn-PiironenVehtari2017RHS}; the
calibration principle is summarized in
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-posterior}.

\subsection{Multiquantile regression (MQ--DESN)}
\label{qdesn2026:subsec:joint-qdesn}

Independent fits at \(K\) ordered levels \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\) assign a
separate quantile regression to each level. MQ--DESN instead uses
level-specific intercepts \(\beta_{0k}\) and slope vectors
\(\vect\beta_k\in\R^r\) on the same feature design:
\begin{equation}
\text{Quantile location:}\quad
\mu_{k,t}=\beta_{0k}+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:joint-qdesn-readout}
\end{equation}
Let \(\vect y=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)^\top\) be the observed response vector,
and let \(\theta\) collect \(\{\beta_{0k},\vect\beta_k,\sigma_k,\gamma_k:
k=1,\ldots,K\}\) together with the shrinkage scales; AL fixes
\(\gamma_k=0\). Write \(\pi(\theta)\) for the joint prior and \(f_{p_k}\)
for the AL or exAL density at level \(p_k\). The composite likelihood and
its associated posterior are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Composite likelihood:}\quad
&L_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)
 =\prod_{t=1}^T\prod_{k=1}^K
 f_{p_k}(y_t\mid\mu_{k,t},\sigma_k,\gamma_k),\\
\text{Composite posterior:}\quad
&\pi_c(\theta\mid\vect y,\mat X)
 \propto\exp\!\left\{-\ell_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)\right\}\pi(\theta).
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:joint-composite-posterior-main}
\end{equation}
Here \(\ell_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)=-\log L_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)\)
is the negative composite log-likelihood. The posterior is defined when its
normalizing constant is finite. Each factor is a normalized density, but the product reuses the same
response across levels and is not a normalized scalar-response sampling
density. It therefore defines a composite, rather than ordinary joint,
posterior for the repeated response. The prior couples levels by regularizing
the anchor block and adjacent slope-difference blocks,
\[
\text{Anchor and differences:}\quad
\vect\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1,\qquad
\vect\Delta_k=\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1},\quad k=2,\ldots,K.
\]
Each \(\vect\Delta_k\) uses an RHS prior with block-specific global and local
scales. Intercepts have separate Gaussian priors and are restricted to an
ordered support in analyses that impose intercept ordering; this restriction
is distinct from shrinkage of the slope differences.
When \(K=1\), the hierarchy
recovers the single-level Q--DESN slope prior under RHS regularization.

Adjacent-difference shrinkage favors similar neighboring slopes but does not
ensure noncrossing: the adjacent location gap
\[
\text{Adjacent location gap:}\quad
\mu_{k,t}-\mu_{k-1,t}
=(\beta_{0k}-\beta_{0,k-1})
+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top(\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1})
\]
can be negative. Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-posterior} gives
the fitted hierarchy and its precision matrix, while
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-computation} records the intercept-order
restrictions used in each analysis.

\section{Posterior computation}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:inference}

Conditional on the design, the posterior combines the likelihood in
Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:qdesn-marginal-main} with the coefficient, scale, and shape
priors. MQ--DESN uses the composite posterior in
Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:joint-composite-posterior-main} and the adjacent-difference
hierarchy. For computation, exAL admits the equivalent
augmentation
\begin{equation}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
\text{Latent variables:}\quad
&v_t\mid\sigma&&\sim\Exp(\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma),
\qquad s_t\sim\TN(0,1),\\
\text{Gaussian conditional:}\quad
&Y_t\mid\cdot&&\sim\Normal\!\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+D_{p_0}(\gamma)\sigma s_t
+A_{p_0}(\gamma)v_t,
B_{p_0}(\gamma)\sigma v_t\right).
\end{alignedat}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:qdesn-aug}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-posterior} defines the
probability-specific functions \(A_{p_0}\), \(B_{p_0}\), and \(D_{p_0}\);
\(D_{p_0}\) is the latent-shift coefficient. Here \(\TN(0,1)\) denotes a
standard Normal distribution truncated to \((0,\infty)\), and the second
Gaussian argument is a variance.
Integrating out \((s_t,v_t)\) recovers the marginal exAL model. For AL,
shape is fixed at zero and the latent shift term is absent.

Conditional on latent variables and prior scales, unconstrained coefficient
blocks have Gaussian full conditional distributions; ordered intercepts have
Gaussian conditionals truncated between neighboring intercepts. Under the
product RHS hierarchy, squared local and global scales, their auxiliaries, and
any random squared slab scale have inverse-gamma conditionals. AL mixture
variables have generalized inverse-Gaussian (GIG) conditionals, and its
likelihood scale has an inverse-gamma conditional. Under exAL, the \(v_t\)
conditionals are GIG and the positive \(s_t\) conditionals are truncated
Normal.

The exAL scale--shape block is nonconjugate. In the reported simulation MCMC,
the shape transition integrates \(\sigma\) out of its conditional kernel,
samples \(\gamma\) from the resulting collapsed density, and immediately
draws \(\sigma\) from its GIG conditional before any other block is changed.
This ordered, partially collapsed transition preserves the intended joint
posterior; interleaving another update between the collapsed shape step and
the scale redraw would not be the same transition. Appendix~%
\ref{app:qdesn-computation} gives the corresponding kernel. Convergence
assessment includes rank-normalized
\(\widehat R\), effective sample sizes, chain comparisons of reported
scores and forecast summaries, and quantile-crossing diagnostics.

Variational Bayes approximates the same model-specific posterior or composite
posterior with tractable factors. It is used for repeated high-dimensional
fits in the applications and as an initialization for some simulation MCMC
fits; the simulation sections identify results based on MCMC and those based
on VB separately. Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-computation} gives the variational
factors, including the Laplace--Delta approximation of
\citet{exd-wang2013nonconjugatevb} and the structured scale--shape factor used
for exAL. Neither objective convergence nor a VB initialization is evidence of
MCMC mixing or exact posterior uncertainty.

\section{Forecasting, scoring, and model selection}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:forecast}

\subsection{Multi-step quantile forecasts}

A forecast origin \(o\) is the last time included in its observed history
and may differ from the fitting endpoint \(T\); horizon \(h\) denotes
prediction of \(Y_{o+h}\). The evaluations retain the fitted parameter
distribution; responses observed after \(T\) initialize the history at later
origins but do not trigger a regression refit. Future covariates are supplied
as the issued forecasts or retrospective paths specified by each study, so
the forecast conditions on those paths rather than propagating uncertainty
about them.

For a parameter draw \(b\), the reservoir is reconstructed through origin
\(o\) from observed responses and covariates. At \(h=1\), all response lags in
Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:desn-input-main} are observed. For \(h>1\), responses
generated earlier on the same trajectory enter the response-lag block before
the next reservoir state is computed. At fitted level \(p\), this path gives
the conditional-quantile location
\[
\mu_{o+h,p}^{(b)}=\beta_{0p}^{(b)}
+\tilde{\vect x}_{o+h}^{(b)\top}\vect\beta_p^{(b)}.
\]
It is conditional on one parameter draw and one recursively generated history.
A marginal posterior predictive quantile first integrates the response law
over parameter and future-path uncertainty and then takes the requested
quantile; it need not equal the average of the conditional locations.

\subsection{Forecast scores}

Write \(\vect q^*=(q_1^*,\ldots,q_K^*)^\top\) for the quantile forecasts at
evaluation levels \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\), including any monotone adjustment
specified for the analysis. In the multiquantile simulation, equal-weight
isotonic projection orders each estimated grid to
address crossings; the projection and scoring range are defined in
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support}.

For a quantile forecast \(q\) at level \(p\), let \(e=y-q\) be the forecast
error and define the check loss by
\begin{equation}
\text{Check loss:}\quad \rho_p(e)=e\{p-\ind(e<0)\}.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:check-loss-main}
\end{equation}
Here \(\ind(e<0)\) is the indicator of a negative error. For a predictive
distribution \(F\) with finite first moment and quantile function \(F^{-1}\),
the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) has the integrated check-loss
representation
\citep{exd-gneiting2007}
\begin{equation}
\text{CRPS:}\quad \operatorname{CRPS}(F,y)
=2\int_0^1\rho_p\{y-F^{-1}(p)\}\,dp.
\label{qdesn2026:eq:crps-integrated-check-main}
\end{equation}
For \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\) and \(K\geq2\), the finite-grid approximate
CRPS (\(\aCRPS\)) applies the trapezoidal rule to
Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:crps-integrated-check-main} over \([p_1,p_K]\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Finite-grid score:}\quad
&\aCRPS_{p_1:p_K}(y;\vect q^*)
 =2\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k\rho_{p_k}(y-q_k^*),\\
\text{Trapezoidal weights:}\quad
&\omega_1=\frac{p_2-p_1}{2},\\
&\omega_k=\frac{p_{k+1}-p_{k-1}}{2},\quad 2\leq k\leq K-1,\\
&\omega_K=\frac{p_K-p_{K-1}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:acrps-main}
\end{equation}
The weights satisfy
\(\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k=p_K-p_1\). Thus \(\aCRPS\) retains the scale of integrated
check loss over the evaluation range rather than representing a full-distribution
CRPS. The evaluation grid is common to the competing methods within each
analysis, not necessarily across analyses.

In the multiquantile simulation, the conditional data-generating process
(DGP) is known. Let
\(\mathcal E=\{(o_\ell,h_\ell):\ell=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{eval}}\}\)
be the \(N_{\mathrm{eval}}\) held-out origin--horizon pairs, and let
\(F_{0,\ell}(y)=\Pr_0(Y_{o_\ell+h_\ell}\le y
\mid\mathcal F_{o_\ell}^{\mathrm{obs}})\)
be the conditional DGP distribution given observed origin history
\(\mathcal F_{o_\ell}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), where \(\Pr_0\) denotes probability
under the DGP. For posterior draw \(b\), let \(q_{\ell,k}^{*,(b)}\) be the
adjusted quantile forecast at level \(p_k\) for pair \(\ell\); the oracle
finite-grid \(\aCRPS\) is
\begin{equation}
\text{Oracle score:}\quad S_{\mathrm{DGP}}^{(b)}
=\frac{2}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k
\int\rho_{p_k}\!\left(y-q_{\ell,k}^{*,(b)}\right)\,dF_{0,\ell}(y).
\label{qdesn2026:eq:dgp-integrated-score-main}
\end{equation}
Here ``oracle'' refers to evaluating expected check loss under the known DGP.
Common simulated DGP paths approximate the expectations over future
responses. For the primary calculation, each model's future predictors are
averaged over simulated response paths before posterior coefficient draws
are evaluated. Score intervals condition on these averaged predictors.
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support} reports the draw-specific
recursive-forecast sensitivity.

\subsection{DESN specification and selection}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:selection}

Candidate DESN specifications vary properties such as depth, layer size,
leak rate, input scaling, and reservoir realization. They are compared using
the observations and criterion specified for each study. Gaussian regressions
on DESN features (Gaussian-DESN) estimate the
conditional mean and provide an inexpensive first comparison through ridge
regularization; where used, Gaussian RHS--VB and quantile
regressions refine the comparison among promising specifications. For a
reference regression \(M\), candidate specifications \(R_1,\ldots,R_J\),
and a selection criterion \(S_{\mathrm{sel}}(R_j;M)\), selection takes
the form
\begin{equation}
\text{Selected reservoir:}\quad \widehat R_M
\in\argmin_{R_j:\,j=1,\ldots,J}
S_{\mathrm{sel}}(R_j;M).
\label{qdesn2026:eq:reservoir-selection-main}
\end{equation}
The selected DESN specification is held fixed for the reported posterior
summaries. In the
multiquantile simulation, the Gaussian-DESN comparison and subsequent refinement select
one specification for each mechanism. Its reservoir realization and feature
construction are shared by independent Q--DESN under AL and exAL and
MQ--DESN under AL and exAL. These four regressions
use the same selected temporal representation while differing in likelihood
and sharing across quantile levels. The single-quantile study uses a separate,
criterion-specific selection procedure.

Checks of the reservoir states assess finite values and saturation near the
limits of bounded activation functions. Checks of the resulting
predictors assess near-constant or highly dependent columns, effective rank,
conditioning, and variation across reservoir realizations. These checks
assess reservoir behavior under the observed inputs and the regression design, complementing the
study of leaky dynamics and the echo state property: the decay of initial-state
effects under a common input history
\citep{qdesn-JaegerLukoseviciusPopoviciSiewert2007LeakyESN,qdesn-YildizJaegerKiebel2012ESP}.

\section{Simulation studies}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:simulation}

\noindent
Two studies examine forecast accuracy and recovery of known conditional
quantile paths. The first follows the error-family comparisons of
\citet{exd-yan2025family}, with a dynamic rather than static quantile path, and
compares single-level Q--DESN with dynamic linear baselines. The second
compares independent Q--DESN and MQ--DESN regressions using the same selected
DESN specification and reservoir realization. We use \(p\) for a single level and \(p_k\), \(k=1,\ldots,K\),
for a grid. Main-text figures report the primary MCMC forecast summaries;
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support} consolidates the longer-window,
calibration, recursive-path, and crossing qualifications.

\subsection{Single-quantile simulation design}

The first study asks whether Q--DESN can recover quantile dynamics generated
by a linear trend and seasonal state model. Observation errors follow
Gaussian, Laplace, or Gaussian-mixture distributions. For each family \(f\), we
consider \(p\in\{0.05,0.25,0.50\}\) and shift the errors so that their
\(p\)-quantile is zero. The observation and state equations are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{State evolution:}\quad
&\vect\theta_{t,f}=\mat G_\theta\vect\theta_{t-1,f}+\vect\eta_{t,f},
\quad \vect\eta_{t,f}\sim\Normal(\zeros{6},\mat W_\theta),\\
\text{Observation:}\quad
&y_{t,p,f}=\vect F^\top\vect\theta_{t,f}+e_{t,f}-H_f^{-1}(p),\\
\text{Conditional quantile:}\quad
&Q_p(Y_{t,p,f}\mid\vect\theta_{t,f})=\vect F^\top\vect\theta_{t,f}.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:single-quantile-dgp-main}
\end{equation}
Here \(H_f\) is the distribution function of the unshifted observation error,
and \(\vect F\) selects the level and seasonal components of the state.
The evolution matrix \(\mat G_\theta\) propagates a local linear trend and
seasonal harmonics, following the construction of \citet{exd-west1997bayesian};
\(\mat W_\theta\) controls their random changes over time. Within each
family, the latent quantile path is shared across the three levels, with only
the error-centering constant changing.

Each model is fitted to 500 observations. The primary evaluation uses the
following 300 observations, giving ten origins and 300 scored origin--horizon
pairs. Forecasts are issued every 30 observations for horizons of up to
30 steps. A sensitivity analysis extends evaluation to the first 500
observations, with the last forecast truncated at that boundary. This shorter
primary period was chosen after examining the longer simulated series. The
resulting comparison is therefore exploratory rather than an independent
confirmation. All methods use the same pairs within each evaluation. Parameters are not
refitted between origins; newly available responses update the states or
recurrent features used for forecasting. The 300 origin--horizon pairs within
a family and quantile level come from one simulated trajectory, not 300
independent simulation replications. Posterior score intervals are therefore
conditional summaries for that trajectory and fitted model.

We compare Q--DESN under AL and exAL likelihoods, both with RHS coefficient
regularization, with DQLM and exDQLM fitted using \pkg{exdqlm}
\citep{env-deleon2026exdqlm}. The dynamic linear baselines use the generating
trend and seasonal structure, which gives them a structural advantage in the
comparison used to assess whether Q--DESN can represent linear temporal
dependence
through recurrent features rather than an explicitly specified state
transition. Separate DESN specifications were selected for the reported
criteria, and forecast performance on the reported evaluation series also
informed earlier refinement. Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-simulation-support}
describes the selection. Consequently, the score comparisons characterize the
selected specifications on these simulated series and should not be interpreted
as performance on an independent confirmation set.

Check loss evaluates the quantile forecasts against realized responses over
the common forecast pairs. The main comparison summarizes its posterior
distribution. Additional results assess recovery of the known
state-conditional quantile path using mean absolute error (MAE), together
with empirical quantile frequencies and fitting-sample recovery.

\subsection{Multiquantile simulation design}

The second simulation study compares MQ--DESN with independent Q--DESN at the
seven levels
\(p_k\in\{0.05,0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,0.90,0.95\}\)
under the \(\AL\) and \(\exAL\) likelihoods. Eight mechanisms vary the
response distribution and temporal dynamics. Within each mechanism, one
DESN and RHS specification is selected using separate simulated series and
held common across the four regressions. Each regression then uses 500
fitting observations, followed by forecast evaluation.

The comparison spans 33 origins and 30 horizons per origin, giving 990 common
origin--horizon pairs from one evaluation trajectory per mechanism. These
pairs are temporally dependent and are not independent simulation replications.
At each origin, observed history initializes the
reservoir, and future responses are generated recursively. Oracle
\(\aCRPS\), defined in Eq.~\eqref{qdesn2026:eq:dgp-integrated-score-main}, evaluates

\section{Application: streamflow quantile forecast correction}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:data}

Daily river-discharge measurements from the United States Geological Survey
(USGS) provide a local reference for assessing hydrological forecasts.
The Global Flood Awareness System (GloFAS) combines meteorological
information with hydrological modeling to produce historical discharge
estimates and ensemble forecasts for flood early warning
\citep{qdesn-AlfieriEtAl2013GloFAS,qdesn-HarriganEtAl2023GloFASForecasts,qdesn-CEMS2026GloFASDocumentation}. Their distributions can differ from those
observed at an individual gauge. We study these differences for the San
Lorenzo River at Big Trees, California, using historical USGS and GloFAS
records to learn quantile-specific discrepancies. Combining this regression
with the issued ensemble gives corrected forecasts of reference-streamflow
quantiles.

\subsection{Data and forecasting problem}

Discharge is analyzed on the \(\log(1+\mathrm{flow})\) scale, with flow
measured in \(\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{s}\) before transformation. Write
\(y_t\) for transformed USGS discharge and \(g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\) for the
transformed historical GloFAS estimate. At the forecast origin \(T\),
\GlofasApplicationCurrentOriginDate{}, GloFAS provides 51 ensemble
members for the following \GlofasApplicationCurrentScoredHorizons{} days,
ending on 22~January~2023. Let \(g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\) denote member
\(e\) at horizon \(h\), on the same transformed scale.

Precipitation predictors come from the Parameter-elevation Regressions on
Independent Slopes Model (PRISM), and soil-moisture predictors from ERA5,
the fifth-generation reanalysis of the European Centre for Medium-Range
Weather Forecasts (ECMWF). Both use realized values during the forecast
window; future USGS measurements are reserved for scoring.
Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-glofas-support} records the historical selection and
forecast-window information boundary.

\subsection{Quantile-based forecast correction}

At probability level \(p\), the gauge and GloFAS regressions share the
reference location \(q_{p,t}\). The GloFAS location adds an adjustment
\(d_{p,t}\) representing its discrepancy from the reference. Let
\(\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\) and \(\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}}\) be predictor
vectors from two fixed DESNs, each including an intercept, with coefficients
\(\vect\beta_p\) and \(\vect\delta_p\). Write
\(\vect y_F=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+H})^\top\) for the unknown reference path
over the \(H=28\) forecast days. Under AL, the historical and forecast-window
likelihoods are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Reference location:}\quad
q_{p,t}&=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\vect\beta_p,\\
\text{Location adjustment:}\quad
d_{p,t}&=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}\top}\vect\delta_p,\\
\text{Reference history:}\quad
y_t\mid\cdot&\sim\AL_p(q_{p,t},\sigma_{Y,p}),\\
\text{Retrospective GloFAS:}\quad
g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\mid\cdot&\sim\AL_p(q_{p,t}+d_{p,t},\sigma_{G,p}),\\
\text{Future reference:}\quad
y_{T+h}\mid\cdot&\sim\AL_p(q_{p,T+h},\sigma_{Y,p}),\\
\text{Issued member:}\quad
g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\mid\cdot&\sim
\AL_p(q_{p,T+h}+d_{p,T+h},\sigma_{G,p}).
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:glofas-part4-main}
\end{equation}
The exAL fits use the corresponding shape-extended likelihood with
source-specific scales and shapes. The historical and issued GloFAS products
share their source parameters, whereas the reference series has its own.

The reference DESN transforms gauge-history and weather inputs. The second
DESN transforms the history of
\(a_t=g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}-y_t\) and weather inputs. Beyond the forecast origin,
both maps require response lags that have not yet been observed. For a
candidate path \(\vect y_F\), the discrepancy reservoir uses earlier values of
\(\bar g_{T+j}^{\mathrm{ens}}-y_{T+j}\), where
\(\bar g_{T+j}^{\mathrm{ens}}=51^{-1}\sum_e g_{T+j,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\).
The ensemble mean constructs a lagged input; it is not a likelihood weight.
Every historical observation, future-reference response, and issued ensemble
member contributes one ordinary likelihood factor. Thus the forecast window
contains 28 latent reference factors and \(28\times51=1428\) member factors.

Because the reference predictors at horizon \(h\) depend only on earlier
responses, the product of future-reference factors defines a normalized
sequential distribution for \(\vect y_F\). The issued members update this
distribution and the regression parameters jointly. No Gaussian
future-path prior replaces the response distribution. The construction carries
uncertainty in unobserved response lags into both DESN predictor vectors.

The comparison uses the seven levels \(0.05\), \(0.20\), \(0.35\), \(0.50\),
\(0.65\), \(0.80\), and \(0.95\). Q--DESN fits the levels separately under
AL or exAL; MQ--DESN applies adjacent-quantile RHS shrinkage within the
reference and discrepancy coefficient paths, retaining a separate future-path
factor for each level. Gaussian DESN baselines with ridge or RHS regularization
and the raw GloFAS ensemble complete the comparison.

The reservoir and prior specifications were selected using historical
development and confirmation periods, without using the held-out issued
window. Compatible earlier fits supply starting moments for the variational
calculation; they do not replace the stated priors. VB alternates regression
updates with a local Gaussian--Delta update of each level-specific future
path. If its retained mean and covariance are \(\vect m_p\) and \(\mat V_p\),
the reported response quantile is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_{F,p}(\vect y_F)&\approx\Normal(\vect m_p,\mat V_p),\\
\widehat q_{p,h}&=m_{p,h}+\Phi^{-1}(p)\sqrt{V_{p,hh}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{qdesn2026:eq:glofas-reference-forecast-main}
\end{equation}
This is the marginal response quantile under the ensemble-conditioned
approximation, not a conditional location evaluated at one generated history.
No additional response-noise term is added. Separate level-specific path
factors can cross, and no post-hoc projection is applied in this application.
Sections~\ref{app:qdesn-computation} and~\ref{app:qdesn-glofas-support}
give the retained covariance terms and numerical stopping criteria.

(Table~\ref{qdesn2026:tab:glofas-application-score}). The quantile-model

\GlofasApplicationCurrentNormalRhsAcrps{} for Gaussian RHS,

\GlofasApplicationCurrentRawAcrps{} for raw GloFAS. Q--DESN under AL has

\section{Application: electricity-price quantile forecasting}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:pricefm-application}

Day-ahead electricity prices vary with demand, renewable generation, and
exchange between connected markets. Conditional quantiles describe the
associated price uncertainty. PriceFM is a pretrained probabilistic forecasting
model that uses regional covariates and transmission-network information
\citep{qdesn-YuEtAl2026PriceFM}.
In leave-one-region-out experiments, its authors report lower average quantile
loss than Chronos, Moirai and TimesFM variants, using matched probability
levels for the TimesFM comparisons. We use the European benchmark to compare
the quantile DESN models with PriceFM forecasts reproduced from the authors'
public code. Version~4 contains day-ahead price records and load, solar, and
wind forecast covariates at 15-minute resolution from 1~January~2022 through
1~January~2026.

\subsection{Comparison design}

The \PricefmFullFolds{} test folds partition 2025 into consecutive,
nonoverlapping four-month blocks: January--April, May--August and
September--December. Evaluating each of the \PricefmFullRegions{} European
bidding regions in every block gives \PricefmFullRegionFolds{} region--fold
evaluations. DESN training begins on 1~January~2022 and expands across folds,
ending at the start of the four-month validation period immediately preceding
each test block. Appendix~\ref{app:qdesn-pricefm-support} gives the exact
boundaries.

For each region, we fit four candidates: Q--DESN and MQ--DESN under AL and
exAL, all with RHS priors. Q--DESN fits the levels separately; MQ--DESN shares
information across levels within a region. Temporal validation periods inside
the first training interval select the reservoir, shrinkage settings and
predictors for each candidate. The first outer validation period selects the
candidate with the lowest AQL. This region-specific choice remains fixed
across all three test folds, while the regression coefficients are refitted
using each fold's training observations. We call the resulting procedure the
selected DESN model.

Historical inputs contain regional prices, load, solar and wind. Selected
spatial specifications also use neighboring-region histories or summaries,
so one regional response can depend on connected-market information without
fitting the regional responses jointly. Future inputs contain the released
load, solar and wind forecasts. These columns are identified as day-ahead
forecasts by \citet{qdesn-YuEtAl2026PriceFM}; their issue-time vintages were
not independently verified. Each daily origin has 96 quarter-hourly targets.
The regressions use direct multihorizon predictors rather than recursively
generated future prices.

Each evaluation is scored on the fold-aligned held-out timestamps and
probability levels \PricefmFullPaperQuantiles. We use average quantile loss
(AQL) to compare forecasts. Throughout this section,
\(\Delta\) AQL denotes selected DESN AQL minus reproduced PriceFM AQL.
Negative values indicate lower DESN AQL; positive values indicate lower
PriceFM AQL.
For region \(r\) and fold \(f\), with \(N_{r,f}\) scored timestamps, the score is
\begin{equation}
\text{Average quantile loss:}\quad
\operatorname{AQL}_{r,f}
=\frac{1}{N_{r,f}K}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r,f}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\rho_{p_k}\!\left(y_{r,f,i}-\widehat q_{r,f,i,k}\right),
\label{qdesn2026:eq:pricefm-aql-main}
\end{equation}
where \(K=7\) and both outcomes and forecasts are in euros per megawatt-hour
(EUR/MWh).
Unlike \(\aCRPS\), AQL equally weights the probability levels and has no
factor of two. Aggregate scores give equal weight to the
\PricefmFullRegionFolds{} region--fold evaluations, rather than pooling all
timestamps.

\subsection{Results}

Mean AQL is \PricefmFullMeanSelectedAql{} for the selected DESN models and
\PricefmFullMeanPricefmAql{} for the reproduced PriceFM forecasts, a reduction
of \PricefmFullMeanReductionAql{} EUR/MWh (5.0\%). The ordering is consistent
across the three fold summaries, whose mean differences range from
\(-0.365\) to \(-0.341\) EUR/MWh
(Table~\ref{qdesn2026:tab:pricefm-matched-aql-comparison}). The comparison is
descriptive: the dates had been examined previously, and the matched forecast
sets do not provide paired uncertainty intervals.

\section{Discussion}
\label{qdesn2026:sec:discussion}

Q--DESN retains a regularized linear regression for the quantile location
while using fixed recurrent features to represent nonlinear temporal
dependence. Its posterior concerns the regression, likelihood, and shrinkage
parameters, conditional on the reservoir. This distinction makes the model
accessible through Bayesian regression calculations without treating every
network weight as an unknown parameter. The AL augmentation supports
Gibbs-based computation, while VB reduces the cost of repeated regression fits.

The exploratory single-quantile comparison is most favorable to Q--DESN at the median,
with likelihood preferences varying by observation-error family. In the
multiquantile study, MQ--DESN improves the ordering of posterior-mean
forecast grids, especially under exAL, while independent regressions have
lower mean oracle scores in most mechanisms. These results distinguish the
benefit of adjacent-coefficient regularization for quantile ordering from its
less consistent gains in forecast scores.

The applications illustrate different uses of the regression. For streamflow
correction, the reference--discrepancy model combines gauge measurements with
GloFAS products at one retrospective forecast origin. All six fitted models
improve on the raw ensemble over the 28 issued horizons, and the four quantile
models have smaller grid \(\aCRPS\) than both Gaussian baselines. Q--DESN under
AL has the lowest grid \(\aCRPS\), \GlofasApplicationCurrentAcrpsReduction{}
below the raw
ensemble. Independent fits perform slightly better within each likelihood:
MQ--DESN scores are approximately 3\% higher under AL and 2\% higher under
exAL. Together with the crossing reductions in the simulations, this suggests
a possible tradeoff between adjacent-coefficient regularization for quantile
coherence and forecast accuracy; it does not establish a universal ordering.

For electricity prices, validation selects among Q--DESN and MQ--DESN under
AL and exAL with RHS priors. The selected models have mean AQL
\PricefmFullMeanSelectedAql{}, compared with
\PricefmFullMeanPricefmAql{} for PriceFM forecasts reproduced from public
code, a reduction of \PricefmFullMeanReductionAql{} EUR/MWh (5.0\%). The
selected DESN mean is lower in all three fold summaries. The applications
retain their stated information sets: realized future weather for streamflow
correction and supplied forecast covariates with unverified issue-time
vintages for electricity prices.

Further work should assess variation across repeated simulated series and
uncertainty from reservoir selection and recursively generated predictors.
Multi-origin GloFAS evaluation with covariates available at issuance and
paired PriceFM uncertainty analyses would extend the present comparisons.
